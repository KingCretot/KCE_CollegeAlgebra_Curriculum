Let's Begin

When the square root is negative

x² = −9 has no real solution, because no real number squared is negative. Mathematicians fixed that by defining a new number, i, whose square is −1. With i, every quadratic equation has solutions, and you'll need that in Lesson 1.4.

Today you'll write square roots of negatives using i, and add, subtract, multiply, and divide complex numbers.

By the end of today, you will be able to:

  • 1Write square roots of negative numbers using i
  • 2Add, subtract, and multiply complex numbers
  • 3Divide complex numbers using the conjugate and simplify powers of i

Vocabulary

The new number system

Imaginary Unit and Complex Numbers

i = √−1, so i² = −1. A complex number has the form a + bi; a is the real part and b the imaginary part. For a > 0, √−a = i√a.

💡Example: √−25 = 5i and √−12 = 2i√3.
Conjugate

The conjugate of a + bi is a − bi. Their product is always a real number: (a + bi)(a − bi) = a² + b².

💡Use: multiplying top and bottom by the denominator's conjugate removes i from the denominator.

How It Works

Operations

1
Rewrite first
Change every √(negative) to i form before multiplying or dividing.
2
Add and subtract
Combine real parts and imaginary parts separately.
3
Multiply
Use FOIL, then replace i² with −1.
4
Divide
Multiply numerator and denominator by the conjugate of the denominator. Powers of i repeat every 4: i, −1, −i, 1.

💭 Notice: √−4 · √−9 is NOT √36 = 6. Rewrite first: 2i · 3i = 6i² = −6.

Real-World Context

Where complex numbers are used

Electrical engineers describe alternating current with complex numbers, and they appear throughout physics and signal processing.

Example: In this course, their main job is giving every quadratic equation a solution: x² + 9 = 0 has solutions ±3i.

Worked Example · Multiplying

(3 + 2i)(4 − 5i)

Goal: Multiply and write in a + bi form.

1
FOIL
12 − 15i + 8i − 10i²
2
Replace i² with −1
12 − 7i + 10
3
Simplify
22 − 7i

Worked Example · Dividing

(2 + i) ÷ (3 − i)

Goal: Write the quotient in a + bi form.

1
Multiply by the conjugate
(2 + i)(3 + i) / [(3 − i)(3 + i)]
2
Numerator
6 + 2i + 3i + i² = 5 + 5i
3
Denominator
9 + 1 = 10
4
Simplify
(5 + 5i)/10 = 1/2 + (1/2)i

Why This Matters

The number system you need for quadratics

The quadratic formula can produce square roots of negatives. Complex numbers let you write those answers instead of stopping at "no real solution."

This shows up everywhere

  • Nonreal solutions of quadratic equations (1.4).
  • Zeros of polynomial functions (Chapter 3).
  • Engineering and physics courses.

Mini Knowledge Test

Show what you know — 5 quick questions

Question 1
Simplify (5 − 3i) − (2 + 4i).
Here's why: Subtract real parts: 5 − 2 = 3. Subtract imaginary parts: −3i − 4i = −7i. 3 + i forgets to distribute the minus sign to 4i.
Question 2
Multiply (2 + 3i)(2 − 3i).
Here's why: These are conjugates: 4 − 6i + 6i − 9i² = 4 + 9 = 13. −5 treats i² as +1.
Question 3
Simplify √−9 · √−4.
Here's why: Rewrite first: 3i · 2i = 6i² = −6. Multiplying under one radical (√36 = 6) is the classic mistake.
Question 4
Simplify i²².
Here's why: 22 ÷ 4 leaves remainder 2, so i²² = i² = −1.
Question 5
Write (4 + 2i)/(1 + i) in a + bi form.
Here's why: Multiply by (1 − i): numerator (4 + 2i)(1 − i) = 4 − 4i + 2i − 2i² = 6 − 2i; denominator 1 + 1 = 2. Result: 3 − i.
