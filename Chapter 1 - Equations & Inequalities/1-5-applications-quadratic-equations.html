Let's Begin

Two answers, one that makes sense

A quadratic equation usually gives two solutions, but a real situation often allows only one. A width can't be negative and a ball can't land before it's thrown. Solving the equation is half the job; deciding which answer fits is the other half.

Today you'll set up and solve area, right-triangle, and projectile problems, and check each solution against the situation.

By the end of today, you will be able to:

  • 1Solve geometry problems that lead to quadratic equations
  • 2Use the Pythagorean theorem in applications
  • 3Solve projectile-height problems and reject solutions that don't fit

Vocabulary

Two key formulas

Pythagorean Theorem

In a right triangle with legs a and b and hypotenuse c: a² + b² = c².

💡Check: the hypotenuse is always the longest side.
Height of a Projectile

s = −16t² + v₀t + s₀, where s is height in feet after t seconds, v₀ is the starting upward speed (ft/sec), and s₀ is the starting height.

💡Ground level: the object hits the ground when s = 0.

How It Works

Solving an application

1
Draw and label
Sketch the situation and name the unknown.
2
Write the equation
Use area, a² + b² = c², or the height formula.
3
Solve
Set equal to 0 and factor, or use the quadratic formula.
4
Reject what doesn't fit
Throw out negative lengths, negative times, and anything else impossible. Answer with units.

Real-World Context

Quadratics in the real world

Landscapers size borders, builders check right angles with the 3-4-5 rule, and physics students track objects thrown upward.

Example: A 10 ft × 14 ft garden gets a border of uniform width x, making a total area of 285 ft²: (10 + 2x)(14 + 2x) = 285 gives x = 2.5 ft.

Worked Example · Area

A rectangle with known area

Goal: A rectangle's length is 4 ft more than its width, and its area is 60 ft². Find its dimensions.

1
Equation
w(w + 4) = 60
2
Standard form
w² + 4w − 60 = 0
3
Factor
(w + 10)(w − 6) = 0 → w = −10 or w = 6
4
Reject and answer
A width can't be negative, so width 6 ft and length 10 ft.

Worked Example · Projectile

A ball thrown from 64 ft up

Goal: s = −16t² + 48t + 64. When does the ball hit the ground, and when is it 96 ft high?

1
Ground: s = 0
−16(t² − 3t − 4) = 0 → (t − 4)(t + 1) = 0
2
Reject t = −1
It hits the ground at t = 4 seconds.
3
Height 96
−16t² + 48t + 64 = 96 → t² − 3t + 2 = 0 → t = 1 or 2
4
Interpret
Both make sense: 96 ft on the way up (1 s) and on the way down (2 s).

Your Turn — Guided Practice

Now you try, with me right here

Set up and decide.

Question 1 — A right triangle has legs x and x + 7 and hypotenuse 13. Find the legs.

Here's why: x² + (x + 7)² = 169 → 2x² + 14x − 120 = 0 → x² + 7x − 60 = 0 → (x + 12)(x − 5) = 0. Lengths are positive, so x = 5: legs 5 and 12.

Question 2 — Solving a height equation gives t = −1.5 and t = 3. What is the answer?

Here's why: Time after the throw can't be negative, so only t = 3 fits the situation.

Mini Knowledge Test

Show what you know — 5 quick questions

Question 1
The product of two consecutive positive integers is 132. What are they?
Here's why: n(n + 1) = 132 → n² + n − 132 = 0 → (n + 12)(n − 11) = 0. Positive, so n = 11: the integers are 11 and 12.
Question 2
A 10 ft × 14 ft garden has a border of uniform width x; the total area is 285 ft². What is x?
Here's why: (10 + 2x)(14 + 2x) = 285 → 4x² + 48x − 145 = 0 → x = [−48 + 68]/8 = 2.5. Check: 15 × 19 = 285 ✓
Question 3
A ball is thrown upward from the ground: s = −16t² + 64t. When does it hit the ground?
Here's why: −16t(t − 4) = 0 gives t = 0 (the launch) or t = 4. It lands at 4 seconds; 2 seconds is when it reaches its peak.
Question 4
Using s = −16t² + 64t, when is the ball 48 ft high?
Here's why: −16t² + 64t = 48 → t² − 4t + 3 = 0 → (t − 1)(t − 3) = 0. Once on the way up and once on the way down.
Question 5
A 25-ft ladder leans against a wall with its base 7 ft from the wall. How high does it reach?
Here's why: h² + 7² = 25² → h² = 625 − 49 = 576 → h = 24 ft.
