Let's Begin

Every point has an address

The rectangular (Cartesian) coordinate system gives every point in the plane an address (x, y). Once points have addresses, you can measure the distance between them, find the point halfway between them, and draw the graph of an equation by plotting the points that make it true.

Today you'll use the distance and midpoint formulas, check whether a point lies on a graph, and find intercepts.

By the end of today, you will be able to:

  • 1Plot points and name the quadrants
  • 2Find the distance between two points and the midpoint of a segment
  • 3Graph an equation by plotting points and finding its x- and y-intercepts

Vocabulary

The words for this lesson

Ordered Pair and Quadrants

(x, y): x is the horizontal position, y the vertical. The axes split the plane into four quadrants, numbered I to IV counterclockwise starting at the upper right.

💡Example: (−3, 2) is in Quadrant II; (5, −4) is in Quadrant IV.
Distance Formula

d = √[(x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²]. It is the Pythagorean theorem applied to the horizontal and vertical legs.

💡Memory hook: difference, square, add, square root.
Midpoint Formula

M = ((x₁ + x₂)/2, (y₁ + y₂)/2). Average the x-values and average the y-values.

💡Example: The midpoint of (−4, 7) and (6, −3) is (1, 2).
Intercepts

The x-intercept is where a graph crosses the x-axis (set y = 0). The y-intercept is where it crosses the y-axis (set x = 0).

💡Memory hook: to find one intercept, set the other variable to 0.

How It Works

Four routines

1
Distance
Subtract x-values, subtract y-values, square both, add, take the square root.
2
Midpoint
Average the x-values; average the y-values.
3
Is a point on the graph?
Substitute the x and y values into the equation. A true statement means yes.
4
Graph an equation
Find the intercepts, make a short table of other points, plot, and connect with the right shape.

💭 Notice: the order of the points doesn't matter in the distance formula, because squaring removes the sign: (5 − (−3))² = (−3 − 5)² = 64.

Real-World Context

Maps are coordinate planes

Map apps place every location on a grid. The straight-line ("as the crow flies") distance between two places is the distance formula, and a fair meeting point halfway between two friends is the midpoint.

Example: On a city grid measured in miles, homes at (1, 2) and (7, 10) are √(36 + 64) = 10 miles apart, and the halfway point is (4, 6).

Worked Example · Distance

From (−3, 2) to (5, −4)

Goal: Find the distance between the points.

1
Differences
x: 5 − (−3) = 8 · y: −4 − 2 = −6
2
Square and add
8² + (−6)² = 64 + 36 = 100
3
Square root
d = √100 = 10
(−3, 2) to (5, −4): across 8, down 6, straight-line distance 10

Worked Example · Midpoint

Midpoint, and working backward

1
Midpoint of (−4, 7) and (6, −3)
((−4 + 6)/2, (7 + (−3))/2) = (2/2, 4/2) = (1, 2)
2
Working backward
The midpoint of a segment is (3, 5) and one endpoint is (1, 2). Find the other endpoint (x, y).
3
Set up each coordinate
(1 + x)/2 = 3 → x = 5 · (2 + y)/2 = 5 → y = 8
4
Answer
The other endpoint is (5, 8). Check: ((1 + 5)/2, (2 + 8)/2) = (3, 5) ✓

Worked Example · Graphing

Intercepts of y = x² − 4

1
y-intercept
Set x = 0: y = 0 − 4 = −4. Point (0, −4).
2
x-intercepts
Set y = 0: x² − 4 = 0 → x = ±2. Points (−2, 0) and (2, 0).
3
More points
x = ±1 → y = −3; x = ±3 → y = 5. Plot and connect with a U-shape.
4
Is (3, 5) on the graph?
3² − 4 = 5 ✓ Yes.
y = x² − 4: x-intercepts (±2, 0), y-intercept (0, −4)

Question 1 — Find the distance from (0, 0) to (3, 4).

Here's why: √(3² + 4²) = √25 = 5. Adding 3 + 4 = 7 skips the squares and the square root.

Question 2 — Is (2, −1) on the graph of y = 3x − 7?

Here's why: Substitute: 3(2) − 7 = 6 − 7 = −1, which matches y = −1. So the point is on the graph.

Mini Knowledge Test

Show what you know — 5 quick questions

Question 1
Find the distance between (1, −2) and (7, 6).
Here's why: √[(7 − 1)² + (6 − (−2))²] = √(36 + 64) = √100 = 10. 100 forgets the square root.
Question 2
Find the midpoint of (−5, 8) and (3, −2).
Here's why: ((−5 + 3)/2, (8 + (−2))/2) = (−2/2, 6/2) = (−1, 3). (−4, 5) is half the difference, not the average.
Question 3
What is the x-intercept of 2x − 3y = 12?
Here's why: Set y = 0: 2x = 12, so x = 6. The x-intercept is (6, 0). (0, −4) is the y-intercept.
Question 4
A segment has midpoint (2, −3) and one endpoint (−1, 4). What is the other endpoint?
Here's why: (−1 + x)/2 = 2 → x = 5. (4 + y)/2 = −3 → y = −10. (0.5, 0.5) is the midpoint of the two given points, not the endpoint.
Question 5
In which quadrant is the point (−4, −7)?
Here's why: Both coordinates are negative: left of the y-axis and below the x-axis. That is Quadrant III.
