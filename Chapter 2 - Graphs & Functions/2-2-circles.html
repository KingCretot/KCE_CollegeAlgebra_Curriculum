Let's Begin

A circle is a distance rule

A circle is every point that is the same distance r from a center (h, k). Write that sentence with the distance formula, square both sides, and you have the equation of a circle. Nothing to memorize that you don't already know from Lesson 2.1.

Today you'll write circle equations, read the center and radius from an equation, and convert from general form by completing the square.

By the end of today, you will be able to:

  • 1Write the center-radius form of a circle from its center and radius
  • 2Find the center and radius from an equation and graph the circle
  • 3Convert general form to center-radius form by completing the square

Vocabulary

Two forms of the equation

Center-Radius Form

(x − h)² + (y − k)² = r². The center is (h, k) and the radius is r.

💡Memory hook: the signs inside flip. (x + 3)² means h = −3.
General Form

x² + y² + Dx + Ey + F = 0. Complete the square in x and in y to get back to center-radius form.

💡Watch: if the right side comes out negative, there is no graph; if it is 0, the graph is a single point.
Unit Circle

x² + y² = 1: center (0, 0), radius 1.

💡Example: x² + y² = 25 has center (0, 0) and radius 5.
Radius from Two Points

If you know the center and one point on the circle, the radius is the distance between them.

💡Example: center (2, −1), point (5, 3): r = √(9 + 16) = 5.

How It Works

From general form to center-radius form

1
Group
Put x-terms together, y-terms together, and move the constant to the right.
2
Complete the square in x
Add (half the x-coefficient)² to both sides.
3
Complete the square in y
Add (half the y-coefficient)² to both sides.
4
Read it off
Factor into (x − h)² + (y − k)² = r². Center (h, k), radius √(right side).

💭 Notice: r² is on the right. If the right side is 20, the radius is √20 = 2√5, not 20.

Real-World Context

Range circles

A cell tower or a radio station reaches everything within a certain distance, so its coverage area is a circle on a map. Emergency planners use circle equations to see which addresses fall inside a range.

Example: A tower at (4, 2) on a grid in miles reaching 10 miles has boundary (x − 4)² + (y − 2)² = 100. A house at (10, 9) is √(36 + 49) = √85 ≈ 9.2 miles away, so it is covered.

Worked Example · General Form

x² + y² − 6x + 10y + 25 = 0

Goal: Find the center and radius.

1
Group
(x² − 6x) + (y² + 10y) = −25
2
Complete both squares
Add 9 and 25 to both sides: (x² − 6x + 9) + (y² + 10y + 25) = −25 + 9 + 25
3
Factor
(x − 3)² + (y + 5)² = 9
4
Read it off
Center (3, −5), radius 3
Center (3, −5), radius 3

Center (2, −1), through (5, 3)

r = √[(5 − 2)² + (3 − (−1))²] = √(9 + 16) = 5

Diameter version

Your Turn — Guided Practice

Now you try, with me right here

Work each one, then tap your answer.

Question 1 — Find the center and radius of (x − 1)² + (y + 2)² = 49.

Here's why: The signs inside flip: x − 1 → h = 1, y + 2 → k = −2. The radius is √49 = 7, not 49.

Question 2 — Does x² + y² + 4x − 2y + 10 = 0 have a graph?

Here's why: (x² + 4x + 4) + (y² − 2y + 1) = −10 + 4 + 1 → (x + 2)² + (y − 1)² = −5. A sum of squares can't be negative, so no points satisfy it.

Why This Matters

Distance, turned into a curve

Circles connect the distance formula to graphing. Completing the square here is the same skill you used for quadratic equations in Lesson 1.4, and you'll use it again for parabolas.

This shows up everywhere

  • Coverage and range problems.

Mini Knowledge Test

Show what you know — 5 quick questions

Question 1
Write the equation of the circle with center (0, 0) and radius 5.
Here's why: (x − 0)² + (y − 0)² = 5² = 25. The right side is r², so 5 must be squared.
Question 2
Find the center and radius of (x + 4)² + (y − 3)² = 20.
Here's why: x + 4 → h = −4 and y − 3 → k = 3. r = √20 = √4 · √5 = 2√5.
Question 3
Find the center and radius of x² + y² + 8x − 6y + 16 = 0.
Here's why: (x² + 8x + 16) + (y² − 6y + 9) = −16 + 16 + 9, so (x + 4)² + (y − 3)² = 9. Center (−4, 3), radius 3.
Question 4
A diameter has endpoints (−1, 2) and (5, 10). Which is the circle's equation?
Here's why: The center is the midpoint (2, 6). The diameter is √(36 + 64) = 10, so r = 5 and r² = 25. Using 10 as the radius gives 100.
Question 5
What is the graph of x² + y² − 2x + 4y + 9 = 0?
Here's why: (x − 1)² + (y + 2)² = −9 + 1 + 4 = −4. A sum of squares can't equal a negative number, so no point satisfies the equation.
