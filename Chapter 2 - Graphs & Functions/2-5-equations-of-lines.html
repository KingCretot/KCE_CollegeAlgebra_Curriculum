Let's Begin

Two facts determine a line

A point and a slope, or two points, are enough to pin down exactly one line. This lesson turns those facts into an equation you can graph, compare, and use to make predictions.

Today you'll write equations in point-slope, slope-intercept, and standard form, and write lines parallel or perpendicular to a given line.

By the end of today, you will be able to:

  • 1Write the equation of a line from a point and a slope, or from two points
  • 2Move between point-slope, slope-intercept, and standard form
  • 3Write lines parallel or perpendicular to a given line, build a linear model from two data points, and solve a linear equation graphically

Vocabulary

Three forms and two relationships

Point-Slope Form

y − y₁ = m(x − x₁), using a known point (x₁, y₁) and slope m.

💡Memory hook: use it whenever you are given a point.
Slope-Intercept Form

y = mx + b: slope m, y-intercept (0, b).

💡Example: y = 4x − 11 has slope 4 and y-intercept (0, −11).
Standard Form

Ax + By = C, with integers A, B, C and A ≥ 0. To read the slope, solve for y: m = −A/B (when B ≠ 0).

💡Example: 3x + 4y = 12 → y = −(3/4)x + 3.
Parallel and Perpendicular

Parallel lines have equal slopes. Perpendicular lines have slopes that are negative reciprocals (product −1).

💡Example: perpendicular to slope 2/3 is slope −3/2.

How It Works

Writing any line

1
Get the slope
Given, computed from two points, or borrowed from a parallel or perpendicular line.
2
Pick a point
Any point the line passes through.
3
Point-slope form
Substitute into y − y₁ = m(x − x₁).
4
Clean up
Solve for y for slope-intercept form, or rearrange to Ax + By = C for standard form.

💭 Notice: vertical lines have undefined slope, so they can't use point-slope or slope-intercept form. A vertical line through (−3, 7) is simply x = −3; a horizontal line through it is y = 7.

Real-World Context

Linear models make predictions

If a quantity grows at a steady rate, a line through two data points predicts future values.

Example: A gym charges a $25 sign-up fee plus $30 per month: C(x) = 30x + 25. After 8 months, C(8) = $265.

Worked Example · Point and Slope

Through (2, −3), slope 4

1
Point-slope
y − (−3) = 4(x − 2) → y + 3 = 4(x − 2)
2
Distribute
y + 3 = 4x − 8
3
Slope-intercept
y = 4x − 11
4
Standard form
4x − y = 11

Worked Example · Two Points

Through (−1, 5) and (3, −3)

1
Slope
m = (−3 − 5)/(3 − (−1)) = −8/4 = −2
2
Point-slope
y − 5 = −2(x + 1)
3
Simplify
y = −2x + 3
4
Check the other point
−2(3) + 3 = −3 ✓

Worked Example · Parallel and Perpendicular

Through (4, 1), related to 2x − y = 5

1
Slope of the given line
2x − y = 5 → y = 2x − 5, so m = 2
2
Parallel: slope 2
y − 1 = 2(x − 4) → y = 2x − 7
3
Perpendicular: slope −1/2
y − 1 = −½(x − 4) → y = −½x + 3
Through (4, 1): parallel (blue) and perpendicular (red) to 2x − y = 5 (gray)

Worked Example · Graphical Solution

The x-intercept method

Goal: Solve 3(x − 2) + 1 = x + 3 graphically, using the x-intercept method.

1
Move everything to one side
3(x − 2) + 1 − (x + 3) = 0, which simplifies to 2x − 8 = 0.
2
Make it a function
Let f(x) = 2x − 8. A solution of the equation is an x-value where f(x) = 0.
3
Find the x-intercept
The graph of f crosses the x-axis at (4, 0), so the solution set is {4}.
4
Check algebraically
3(4 − 2) + 1 = 7 and 4 + 3 = 7 ✓
y = 2x − 8 crosses the x-axis at (4, 0)

💭 Notice: the x-intercept of f is the solution. The y-intercept (0, −8) is not.

Worked Example · Modeling

A line through two data points

Goal: A college's yearly tuition was $3,000 in 2016 and $3,600 in 2022 (illustration data). Assume a linear change. Write a model and predict the tuition in 2027.

1
Choose x
Let x = years since 2016. The data points are (0, 3000) and (6, 3600).
2
Slope
m = (3600 − 3000)/(6 − 0) = 600/6 = 100: tuition rises $100 per year.
3
Equation
The y-intercept is (0, 3000), so y = 100x + 3000.
4
Predict
2027 is x = 11: y = 100(11) + 3000 = $4,100.

💭 Notice: choosing x as "years since" the first year makes the first data point the y-intercept, which keeps the numbers small.

Your Turn — Guided Practice

Now you try, with me right here

Work each one, then tap your answer.

Question 1 — What are the slope and y-intercept of 3x + 4y = 12?

Here's why: Solve for y: 4y = −3x + 12, so y = −(3/4)x + 3. The coefficients of standard form are not the slope.

Question 2 — What slope is perpendicular to y = 5x − 2?

Here's why: Flip and change the sign: the negative reciprocal of 5 is −1/5. Check: 5 · (−1/5) = −1.

Question 3 — Use the x-intercept method to solve 2x + 5 = x + 2. Where does f(x) = x + 3 cross the x-axis?

Here's why: 2x + 5 − (x + 2) = x + 3. Set f(x) = x + 3 = 0: x = −3. The graph crosses at (−3, 0). Check: 2(−3) + 5 = −1 and −3 + 2 = −1 ✓

Question 4 — A plant is 4 cm tall on day 2 and 10 cm tall on day 5. Assuming linear growth, predict its height on day 9.

Here's why: Slope = (10 − 4)/(5 − 2) = 2 cm per day. y − 4 = 2(x − 2), so y = 2x. On day 9, y = 18 cm. 22 comes from using 4 as the y-intercept, but 4 cm is the height on day 2, not day 0.

Why This Matters

Lines are the workhorse model

Mini Knowledge Test

Show what you know — 7 quick questions

Question 1
Write the line through (1, 4) with slope −3.
Here's why: y − 4 = −3(x − 1) → y = −3x + 3 + 4 = −3x + 7. Using 4 as the intercept skips the point-slope step.
Question 2
Write the line through (2, 1) and (6, 9).
Here's why: m = (9 − 1)/(6 − 2) = 2. y − 1 = 2(x − 2) → y = 2x − 3. Check (6, 9): 12 − 3 = 9 ✓
Question 3
Write the line through (0, 6) parallel to y = −4x + 1.
Here's why: Parallel means the same slope, −4. The point (0, 6) is the y-intercept, so y = −4x + 6.
Question 4
Write the line through (2, 5) perpendicular to y = (2/3)x + 1.
Here's why: Perpendicular slope: −3/2. y − 5 = −(3/2)(x − 2) = −(3/2)x + 3, so y = −(3/2)x + 8.
Question 5
Which is the equation of the vertical line through (−3, 7)?
Here's why: A vertical line keeps x fixed, so every point has x = −3. y = 7 is the horizontal line through the same point.
Question 6
To solve 4x − 3 = 2x + 5 graphically, which function's x-intercept gives the solution?
Here's why: Subtract the right side: 4x − 3 − (2x + 5) = 2x − 8. Its x-intercept is (4, 0), so the solution set is {4}. The other choices add 2x instead of subtracting it, or subtract the 5 with the wrong sign.
Question 7
A store sold 120 units in month 1 and 180 units in month 4. Assuming a linear trend, predict the sales in month 10.
Here's why: Slope = (180 − 120)/(4 − 1) = 20 units per month. y − 120 = 20(x − 1), so y = 20x + 100 and y(10) = 300. 320 uses 120 as the y-intercept; 200 drops the intercept; 660 forgets to divide 60 by 3.
