Let's Begin

Find k, and the rest follows

Double your hours and your paycheck doubles. Double your speed and the drive takes half the time. Both are variation: one quantity is a constant multiple of another, or of its reciprocal. That constant, the constant of variation k, is the key to every problem.

Today you'll translate variation statements into equations, find the constant of variation, and use it to answer questions.

By the end of today, you will be able to:

  • 1Write equations for direct, inverse, joint, and combined variation
  • 2Find the constant of variation k from one set of values
  • 3Solve applied variation problems, including powers like x² and 1/x²

Vocabulary

The words for this lesson

Direct Variation

y varies directly as x when y = kx for a nonzero constant k. The same statement can be said as “y is directly proportional to x” or “y is proportional to x.” “Directly as the nth power of x” means y = kxⁿ.

💡Memory hook: y = kx is a line through the origin with slope k. Double x, double y.
Inverse Variation

y varies inversely as x when y = k/x for a nonzero constant k. Also said: “y is inversely proportional to x.” As x grows (with k > 0), y shrinks. “Inversely as the square of x” means y = k/x².

💡Example: time to drive 120 miles at r mph: t = 120/r. Double the speed, half the time.
Joint Variation

y varies jointly as x and z when y = kxz: y is a constant times the product of the variables. Also said: “y is jointly proportional to x and z.”

💡Example: the area of a triangle, A = ½bh, varies jointly as b and h, with k = ½.
Combined Variation

Direct and inverse variation in one equation. Direct variables go on top of the fraction; inverse variables go on the bottom.

💡Example: “w varies directly as x and inversely as y” means w = kx/y.
Direct: y = 2x (a line through the origin)
Inverse: y = 6/x (as x grows, y shrinks)

How It Works

Solving any variation problem in four steps

1
Translate
Turn the words into an equation with k. “Directly” puts a variable on top, “inversely” puts it on the bottom, “jointly” multiplies, and “the square of” adds an exponent. “Is proportional to” means the same as “varies directly as,” and “is inversely proportional to” means “varies inversely as.”
2
Find k
Substitute the one complete set of values you are given and solve for k.
3
Rewrite
Put the value of k back into the equation. Now you have a formula that works for every case.
4
Answer
Substitute the new values and solve for the quantity asked for. Include units.

💭 Notice: k is found once, from the first set of values, and then reused. If you skip finding k, you have no formula, only a guess.

Real-World Context

Stopping distance and the square of speed

A car's braking distance varies directly as the square of its speed: d = kv². Suppose a car moving at 30 mph needs 45 feet to stop on dry pavement. How far does it need at 60 mph?

d = 0.05v²: each square is 10 mph by 20 ft

Example: k = 45/30² = 45/900 = 0.05, so d = 0.05v². At 60 mph, d = 0.05(3600) = 180 feet. Doubling the speed takes four times the distance: 180 feet.

Worked Example · Direct Variation

3 gallons of paint cover 1050 ft². How much for 1750 ft²?

1
Translate
Paint varies directly as wall area: g = kA, with g in gallons and A in square feet.
2
Find k
3 = k(1050), so k = 3/1050 = 1/350.
3
Answer
g = 1750/350 = 5 gallons.

💭 Notice: Check that the answer makes sense: more wall needs more paint. If your answer came out smaller than 3 gallons, you set up inverse variation by mistake.

Worked Example · Inverse Square

A lamp gives 320 lux at 2 m. How bright is it at 4 m?

1
Translate
Light intensity varies inversely as the square of the distance: I = k/d².
2
Find k
320 = k/2² = k/4, so k = 1280.
3
Answer
I = 1280/4² = 1280/16 = 80 lux. Doubling the distance cuts the light to one fourth.

💭 Common slip: Forgetting the square gives k = 640 and I = 640/4 = 160 lux, twice the real answer. Inverse square means doubling the distance divides by 2² = 4.

Worked Example · Joint Variation

Kinetic energy is jointly proportional to mass and the square of speed

Goal: The kinetic energy E of a moving object is jointly proportional to its mass m and the square of its speed v. A 2-kilogram ball moving 3 meters per second has 9 joules of kinetic energy. Find the kinetic energy of a 4-kilogram ball moving 5 meters per second.

1
Translate
“Jointly proportional” means “varies jointly,” so multiply: E = kmv². Only v is squared.
2
Find k
9 = k(2)(3²) = 18k, so k = 9/18 = 1/2. The formula is E = ½mv², the one used in physics.
3
Answer
E = ½(4)(5²) = ½(4)(25) = 50 joules.

💭 Common slip: Squaring the mass too, or forgetting to square the speed. Square only the variable the words say: “the square of its speed.” Without the square, k = 9/6 = 1.5 and the answer would be 1.5(4)(5) = 30 joules, which is wrong.

Worked Example · Combined Variation

V varies directly as T and inversely as P

1
Translate
A gas has V = 300 cm³ when T = 250 K and P = 50 kPa. Find V when T = 300 K and P = 40 kPa. The equation is V = kT/P: T on top (direct), P on the bottom (inverse).
2
Find k
300 = k(250)/50 = 5k, so k = 60.
3
Answer
V = 60(300)/40 = 18,000/40 = 450 cm³.

💭 Common slip: Write the equation before plugging anything in. Most errors in combined variation come from putting a variable on the wrong side of the fraction bar.

Your Turn — Guided Practice

Now you try, with me right here

Work each one, then tap your answer.

Question 1 — z varies jointly as x and y, and z = 60 when x = 3 and y = 4. Find z when x = 5 and y = 2.

Here's why: z = kxy. 60 = k(3)(4) = 12k, so k = 5. Then z = 5(5)(2) = 50. 10 is just 5 · 2: it skips finding k.

Question 2 — y varies inversely as x, and y = 8 when x = 3. Find y when x = 12.

Here's why: y = k/x, so k = 8 · 3 = 24 and y = 24/12 = 2. 32 comes from treating it as direct variation (k = 8/3, then y = 8/3 · 12). Inverse means y gets smaller as x grows.

Why This Matters

Variation is how laws of nature are written

Many science formulas are variation statements in disguise. Once you can read “directly,” “inversely,” and “jointly,” you can set up and solve problems from physics, chemistry, and business with the same four steps.

This shows up everywhere

  • Hooke's law (a spring's stretch varies directly as the force), gravity (inverse square), and the gas laws (combined).
  • Inverse variation y = k/x is the rational function from Lesson 3.5: same shape, same asymptotes.
  • Direct variation as a power, y = kxⁿ, is the simplest polynomial model from Lesson 3.4.

Mini Knowledge Test

Show what you know — 7 quick questions

Question 1
y varies directly as x, and y = 18 when x = 4. Find y when x = 10.
Here's why: y = kx, so k = 18/4 = 4.5 and y = 4.5(10) = 45. 7.2 treats it as inverse (k = 72, then 72/10), 24 just adds 6 because x went up by 6, and 180 multiplies 18 by 10 without finding k.
Question 2
I varies inversely as the square of d, and I = 50 when d = 3. Find I when d = 6.
Here's why: I = k/d², so k = 50 · 9 = 450 and I = 450/36 = 12.5. 25 forgets the square (k = 150, then 150/6), 200 treats it as direct variation with the square, and 100 treats it as plain direct variation.
Question 3
Which equation says “w varies jointly as x and y and inversely as the square of z”?
Here's why: Jointly means multiply x and y on top; inversely puts z on the bottom; “the square of” makes it z². So w = kxy/z². Jointly never means add.
Question 4
y varies directly as x and inversely as z, and y = 12 when x = 8 and z = 2. Find y when x = 6 and z = 3.
Here's why: y = kx/z, so 12 = 8k/2 = 4k and k = 3. Then y = 3(6)/3 = 6. 13.5 puts z on top (y = kxz), 24 swaps x and z, and 2 skips finding k (6/3).
Question 5
y varies directly as the square of x. If x is tripled, what happens to y?
Here's why: y = kx². Replace x with 3x: k(3x)² = 9kx² = 9y. Multiplying by 3 forgets the square, 6 doubles the 3 instead of squaring it, and dividing is what inverse variation would do.
Question 6
y is directly proportional to x and inversely proportional to the square of z. If y = 10 when x = 5 and z = 2, find y when x = 6 and z = 4.
Here's why: y = kx/z². Then 10 = k(5)/2² = 5k/4, so k = 8, and y = 8(6)/4² = 48/16 = 3. 6 forgets the square (y = kx/z), 48 puts z² on top (y = kxz²), and 24 treats both variables as direct (y = kxz).
Question 7
w varies jointly as x and the square of y. If w = 48 when x = 3 and y = 2, find w when x = 2 and y = 5.
Here's why: w = kxy². Then 48 = k(3)(2²) = 12k, so k = 4, and w = 4(2)(5²) = 4(2)(25) = 200. 80 forgets the square (k = 8, then 8 · 2 · 5), 5.12 divides by y² as if the variation were inverse, and 50 skips finding k (2 · 25).
