Let's Begin

When the variable moves into the exponent

A video has 100 views, and its view count doubles every hour. Ten hours later it has 102,400. Doubling, halving, and interest on savings all follow an exponential function, where the variable sits in the exponent and the change multiplies instead of adds.

Today you'll graph exponential functions, shift them around, solve equations by matching bases, and compute compound and continuous interest.

By the end of today, you will be able to:

  • 1Graph f(x) = ax and its transformations, naming the asymptote, domain, and range
  • 2Solve exponential equations by writing both sides with the same base
  • 3Use A = P(1 + r/n)nt and A = Pert to find compound and continuous interest
  • 4Find present value, evaluate and interpret an exponential model, and key both into a TI-30XIIS

Vocabulary

The words for this lesson

Exponential Function

f(x) = ax, where the base a > 0 and a ≠ 1. Domain (−∞, ∞), range (0, ∞).

💡Memory hook: linear functions add the same amount each step; exponential functions multiply by the same factor.
Growth vs. Decay

If a > 1 the graph rises left to right (growth). If 0 < a < 1 it falls (decay). Both pass through (0, 1).

💡Example: 2x grows; (½)x decays.
Horizontal Asymptote

The graph of ax gets closer and closer to y = 0 but never touches it. Shifting up or down moves the asymptote with it.

💡Watch the sign: 2x − 3 has asymptote y = −3, not y = 0.
The Number e

An irrational number, e ≈ 2.71828. It is the base used for continuous growth: A = Pert.

💡Memory hook: Pert sounds like “pert,” the continuous-interest formula.
Growth: f(x) = 2x
Decay: f(x) = (½)x

How It Works

Graphing and shifting ax

1
Exponent rules recap
am · an = am+n, (am)n = amn, a−n = 1/an, and a0 = 1. You will use (am)n = amn most today.
2
Plot the anchor points
Every f(x) = ax passes through (0, 1) and (1, a), with asymptote y = 0.
3
Apply the shifts
f(x) = ax − h + k moves the graph right h and up k. The anchor (0, 1) moves to (h, 1 + k) and the asymptote becomes y = k.
4
State domain and range
Domain is always (−∞, ∞). The range is (k, ∞) when the graph sits above its asymptote.
f(x) = 2x − 1 − 3 (dashed: parent 2x)

💭 Notice: A horizontal shift never changes the asymptote. Only the vertical shift k moves it.

Real-World Context

Money that grows every instant

Banks compound interest n times a year: A = P(1 + r/n)nt. As n grows (monthly, daily, every second) the amount approaches a limit, and that limit is continuous compounding, A = Pert. Here P is the principal, r the annual rate as a decimal, and t the time in years.

Example: $2,000 invested at 3.5% compounded continuously for 10 years: A = 2000e0.035 · 10 = 2000e0.35 ≈ 2000(1.41907) ≈ $2,838.14. Compounded once a year it would be 2000(1.035)10 ≈ $2,821.20, about $17 less.

Worked Example · Matching Bases

Solve 42x − 1 = 8x + 1

1
Rewrite with base 2
4 = 2² and 8 = 2³, so (2²)2x − 1 = (2³)x + 1.
2
Multiply the exponents
24x − 2 = 23x + 3. Same base, so the exponents must be equal: 4x − 2 = 3x + 3.
3
Solve and check
x = 5. Check: 49 = 218 and 86 = 218.

💭 Common slip: Distribute to every term in the exponent: (2²)2x − 1 is 24x − 2, not 24x − 1.

Worked Example · Transformations

Graph f(x) = 3x + 2 − 1

1
Read the shifts
x + 2 means h = −2 (left 2). The −1 means down 1.
2
Move the anchors
(0, 1) on 3x moves to (−2, 0); (1, 3) moves to (−1, 2). The y-intercept is f(0) = 3² − 1 = 8.
3
Asymptote, domain, range
Asymptote y = −1. Domain (−∞, ∞). Range (−1, ∞).
f(x) = 3x + 2 − 1

💭 Notice: “x + 2” moves the graph left, not right. Same rule as Lesson 2.7.

Worked Example · Compound Interest

$5,000 at 4% compounded quarterly for 6 years

1
Identify the values
P = 5000, r = 0.04, n = 4 (quarterly), t = 6.
2
Substitute
A = 5000(1 + 0.04/4)4 · 6 = 5000(1.01)24.
3
Compute
(1.01)24 ≈ 1.269735, so A ≈ $6,348.67. The interest earned is about $1,348.67.

💭 Common slip: Divide the rate by n and multiply the time by n. Using (1.04)24 treats 4% as the quarterly rate and wildly overstates the answer.

Worked Example · Present Value

How much must you deposit today to have $10,000 in 5 years?

The account pays 4% interest compounded monthly. This time you know the future amount A and need the starting amount P, called the present value.

1
Solve the formula for P
Divide both sides of A = P(1 + r/n)nt by (1 + r/n)nt: P = A / (1 + r/n)nt.
2
Substitute
A = 10,000, r = 0.04, n = 12, t = 5: P = 10,000 / (1 + 0.04/12)60.
3
Compute and round
(1 + 0.04/12)60 ≈ 1.220997, so P ≈ 10,000 / 1.220997 ≈ $8,190.03. Deposit about $8,190.03 today.

TI-30XIIS keystrokes: 10000 ÷ ( 1 + 0.04 ÷ 12 ) ^ ( 12 × 5 ) ENTER. The display shows 8190.031037. Put parentheses around the base and around the exponent nt, or the calculator raises only part of the expression to the power.

💭 Common slip: Present value is always less than the future amount. If your answer is bigger than $10,000, you multiplied by (1 + r/n)nt instead of dividing.

Worked Example · Exponential Model

Evaluate and interpret f(t) = 15e0.04t

The population of a town, in thousands, t years after 2020 is modeled by f(t) = 15e0.04t. Estimate the population in 2025.

1
Find t
2025 is 5 years after 2020, so t = 5. (Do not plug in 2025.)
2
Evaluate
f(5) = 15e0.04 · 5 = 15e0.2 ≈ 15(1.221403) ≈ 18.321.
3
Interpret with units
f is in thousands, so the model predicts about 18,321 people in 2025. The 15 means 15,000 people in 2020, since f(0) = 15e0 = 15.

TI-30XIIS keystrokes: 15 × 2nd LN (this is the ex key, and the screen shows e^( ) 0.04 × 5 ) ENTER. The display shows 18.32104137.

💭 Common slip: Multiply 0.04 by t before you take e to that power. 15e0.04 · 5 is a different number.

Your Turn — Guided Practice

Now you try, with me right here

Work each one, then tap your answer.

Question 1: Solve 3x + 1 = 81.

Here's why: 81 = 34, so x + 1 = 4 and x = 3. The answer x = 4 stops at the exponent 4 and forgets to subtract the 1.

Question 2: Does f(x) = 5(0.6)x model growth or decay?

Here's why: The base is the number raised to the x, here 0.6. A base between 0 and 1 means decay. The 5 is only the starting value f(0) = 5; it does not decide growth or decay.

Question 3: You want $8,000 in 4 years. The account pays 3% compounded quarterly. What is the present value?

Here's why: P = 8,000 / (1 + 0.03/4)16 = 8,000 / (1.0075)16 ≈ $7,098.54. $9,015.94 multiplies instead of dividing, which gives the future value of $8,000, not the present value. $7,107.90 compounds once a year, 8,000 / (1.03)4.

Why This Matters

The shape behind growth, decay, and interest

Populations, savings accounts, medicine leaving the body, and viral posts all change by a percent, not a fixed amount. The exponential function is the model for all of them, and the rest of Chapter 4 is about answering questions those models raise.

This shows up everywhere

  • Lesson 4.3 introduces logarithms, the inverse of ax, for when the unknown is in the exponent and the bases do not match.
  • Lesson 4.6 uses A(t) = A0ekt for half-life, doubling time, and population growth.
  • Loans and credit cards use the same compound interest formula, working against you.

Mini Knowledge Test

Show what you know: 7 quick questions

Question 1
What are the asymptote and range of f(x) = 2x + 3?
Here's why: Adding 3 lifts the graph and its asymptote up 3, so y = 3 and the graph stays above it: range (3, ∞). Keeping y = 0 ignores the shift, and −3 flips its direction.
Question 2
Solve 25x = 125x − 2.
Here's why: Base 5: 52x = 53x − 6, so 2x = 3x − 6 and x = 6. x = 2 forgets to distribute the 3 (writing 3x − 2), −6 is a sign slip, and 6/5 comes from adding 2x and 3x instead of subtracting.
Question 3
$1,200 is invested at 6% compounded monthly. How much is in the account after 3 years?
Here's why: A = 1200(1 + 0.06/12)36 = 1200(1.005)36 ≈ $1,436.02. $1,416.00 is simple interest, $1,429.22 compounds only once a year, and $9,776.70 forgets to divide 6% by 12.
Question 4
$800 is invested at 5% compounded continuously. How much is there after 4 years?
Here's why: A = 800e0.05 · 4 = 800e0.2 ≈ $977.12. $972.41 compounds yearly, $960.00 is simple interest, and $841.02 forgets to multiply the rate by t.
Question 5
Which statement describes the graph of f(x) = (1/3)x?
Here's why: The base 1/3 is between 0 and 1, so the graph decays (falls). Any a0 = 1, so it passes through (0, 1), not (0, 1/3). The asymptote is horizontal, y = 0.
Question 6
A car’s value, in dollars, t years after purchase is V(t) = 24,000(0.85)t. Find V(6) to the nearest dollar.
Here's why: V(6) = 24,000(0.85)6 ≈ 24,000(0.377150) ≈ $9,052, so the car is worth about $9,052 after 6 years. $2,400 subtracts 15% of the original price each year, which is linear, not exponential. $55,513 uses 1.15, which would be growth. $10,649 is V(5), one year short.
Question 7
How much must be deposited today at 5% compounded semiannually to have $15,000 in 6 years? Round to the nearest cent.
Here's why: P = 15,000 / (1 + 0.05/2)12 = 15,000 / (1.025)12 ≈ $11,153.34. $11,193.23 compounds annually, $20,173.33 multiplies instead of dividing, and $12,934.45 uses an exponent of 6 instead of nt = 12.
