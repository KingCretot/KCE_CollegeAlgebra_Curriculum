Let's Begin

Any logarithm, on any calculator

Your calculator has only two log keys: LOG for base 10 and LN for base e. Yet chemists, sound engineers, and test writers ask for logs in every base. The change-of-base theorem turns any logarithm into one of those two keys.

Today you'll evaluate common and natural logarithms, use them in pH and decibel formulas, change any log to base 10 or base e, and find the domain of a log function.

By the end of today, you will be able to:

  • 1Evaluate common and natural logarithms and use them in pH and decibel models
  • 2Use the change-of-base theorem to evaluate loga x for any base a
  • 3Find the domain of a logarithmic function

Vocabulary

The words for this lesson

Common Logarithm

log x means log10 x. When no base is written, the base is 10. It is the LOG key.

💡Example: log 1000 = 3 because 10³ = 1000.
Natural Logarithm

ln x means loge x, where e ≈ 2.718. It is the LN key.

💡Memory hook: ln e = 1 and ln e5 = 5, just as log 10 = 1.
Change-of-Base Theorem

For any positive a ≠ 1 and x > 0: loga x = ln x / ln a = log x / log a.

💡Memory hook: the base goes to the basement: the base sits in the denominator.
pH

pH = −log[H3O+], where [H3O+] is the hydronium ion concentration in moles per liter.

💡Direction: pH below 7 is acidic, 7 is neutral, above 7 is basic.
y = ln x (blue) and y = log x (red) both pass through (1, 0)

How It Works

Evaluating logs on a calculator

1
Common log
Type log, then the number. log 250 ≈ 2.3979, which means 102.3979 ≈ 250.
2
Natural log
Type ln, then the number. ln 250 ≈ 5.5215, which means e5.5215 ≈ 250.
3
Any other base
Change the base: loga x = ln x / ln a. Divide the two logs, and round only the final answer.
4
Domain
You can only take the log of a positive number. For f(x) = loga(expression), solve expression > 0.

💭 Notice: ln x / ln a and log x / log a always give the same answer, so use whichever key you like. Just keep both logs in the same base.

Real-World Context

How loud is a leaf blower?

Sound intensity spans a huge range, so it is measured with a common log. The decibel rating of a sound with intensity I is D = 10 log(I / I0), where I0 is the faintest sound a person can hear. A leaf blower has an intensity of about 3,000,000,000 I0.

Example: D = 10 log(3,000,000,000) ≈ 10(9.477) = 94.77. To the nearest whole decibel, the leaf blower is about 95 decibels.

Worked Example · Common Log · pH

Find the pH of a solution with [H3O+] = 4.7 × 10−9

1
Write the formula
pH = −log[H3O+] = −log(4.7 × 10−9)
2
Evaluate
log(4.7 × 10−9) ≈ −8.3279, so pH ≈ −(−8.3279) = 8.3279
3
Round and interpret
To the nearest tenth, pH = 8.3. That is above 7, so the solution is basic.

💭 Common slip: On the TI-30XIIS, press LOG and enter 4.7 × 10 ^ (−) 9, then close the parenthesis (or use the EE key: 4.7E(−)9). Use the (−) key for the negative exponent, not the subtraction key.

Worked Example · Change of Base

Evaluate log5 80 to the nearest hundredth

1
Change the base
log5 80 = ln 80 / ln 5
2
Evaluate each log
ln 80 ≈ 4.3820 and ln 5 ≈ 1.6094
3
Divide, then round
4.3820 / 1.6094 ≈ 2.7227, so log5 80 ≈ 2.72. Check: 52.72 ≈ 79.7, close to 80.

💭 Common slip: The number you are taking the log of goes on top. ln 5 / ln 80 ≈ 0.37 is upside down, and a quick check (50.37 is about 1.8, not 80) catches it.

Worked Example · Domain

Find the domain of f(x) = ln(5 − 2x)

1
Set the argument > 0
5 − 2x > 0
2
Solve
−2x > −5, so x < 5/2. Dividing by −2 flips the inequality.
3
Write the interval
Domain: (−∞, 5/2). The line x = 5/2 is a vertical asymptote.
f(x) = ln(5 − 2x), asymptote x = 5/2

💭 Common slip: The argument must be strictly greater than 0, not ≥ 0. Since ln 0 is undefined, 5/2 is left out and the interval uses a parenthesis.

Your Turn — Guided Practice

Now you try, with me right here

Work each one, then tap your answer.

Question 1 — Evaluate log3 40 to the nearest hundredth.

Here's why: log3 40 = ln 40 / ln 3 ≈ 3.6889 / 1.0986 ≈ 3.36. Check: 33.36 ≈ 40. 0.30 comes from dividing upside down, ln 3 / ln 40. The base belongs in the denominator.

Question 2 — What is the domain of f(x) = log(x − 7)?

Here's why: x − 7 > 0 gives x > 7, so the domain is (7, ∞). [7, ∞) includes x = 7, but log(7 − 7) = log 0 is undefined. The argument must be strictly positive.

Why This Matters

Logs measure things that span huge ranges

Acidity, loudness, and earthquake strength all vary by factors of millions. A logarithm squeezes that range into numbers people can compare, and the change-of-base theorem lets you compute in any base with the two keys you have.

This shows up everywhere

  • Chemistry and biology use pH; acoustics uses decibels; seismology uses the Richter scale.
  • Lesson 4.5 solves exponential equations like 5x = 80, and the answer is exactly log5 80.
  • Lesson 4.6 uses ln to find growth rates, half-lives, and doubling times.

Mini Knowledge Test

Show what you know — 5 quick questions

Question 1
Find the pH of a solution with [H3O+] = 6.0 × 10−3, to the nearest tenth.
Here's why: pH = −log(0.006) ≈ −(−2.2218) = 2.2. −2.2 forgets the negative sign in the formula, 5.1 uses ln instead of log, and 0.8 is log 6, which ignores the power of 10.
Question 2
Evaluate log7 25 to the nearest hundredth.
Here's why: log7 25 = ln 25 / ln 7 ≈ 3.2189 / 1.9459 ≈ 1.65. 0.60 divides upside down, 3.57 is just 25 ÷ 7, and 0.55 is log(25/7), which is not the same as dividing two logs.
Question 3
What is the domain of g(x) = ln(x + 6)?
Here's why: x + 6 > 0 gives x > −6, so the domain is (−6, ∞). The bracket wrongly includes ln 0, and (6, ∞) flips the sign.
Question 4
A sound has intensity 50,000 I0. Use D = 10 log(I / I0) to find its rating to the nearest decibel.
Here's why: D = 10 log(50,000) ≈ 10(4.699) ≈ 47. 4.7 forgets to multiply by 10, 108 uses ln instead of log, and 40 just counts the four zeros.
Question 5
Which expression is equal to log4 30?
Here's why: Change of base puts the log of the number on top and the log of the base on the bottom, both in the same base: ln 30 / ln 4. ln(30/4) is a quotient inside one log, and log 30 / ln 4 mixes two different bases.
