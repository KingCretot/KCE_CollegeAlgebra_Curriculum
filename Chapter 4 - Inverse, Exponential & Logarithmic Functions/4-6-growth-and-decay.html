Let's Begin

Half-life, doubling time, and the number k

A town grows, a medicine fades from the bloodstream, a savings account builds interest. Each one changes at a rate proportional to its size, so each follows the same exponential model A(t) = A0ekt. Find one number, k, and the model answers every question about the future.

Today you'll build exponential models from data, use half-life and doubling time, and answer “how much?” and “how long?” questions with ln.

By the end of today, you will be able to:

  • 1Find the constant k in A(t) = A0ekt from data and use the model to predict
  • 2Solve half-life and decay problems, including carbon dating
  • 3Find doubling times for growth and continuous compounding
  • 4Use Newton’s law of cooling, f(t) = T0 + Ce−kt, to find C and k from data and predict temperatures and times

Vocabulary

The words for this lesson

Exponential Model

A(t) = A0ekt, where A0 is the amount at time t = 0 and k is a constant.

💡Direction: k > 0 means growth; k < 0 means decay.
Growth or Decay Constant k

The continuous rate in the model. From a data point, k = ln(A / A0) / t.

💡Example: k = 0.03 means growing continuously at 3% per unit of time.
Half-Life

The time it takes for half of a decaying quantity to disappear. If the half-life is h, then k = −ln 2 / h.

💡Memory hook: after each half-life, cut the amount in half again.
Doubling Time

The time it takes a growing quantity to double: t = ln 2 / k.

💡Watch the units: t comes out in whatever time unit k uses.

How It Works

Building and using a model

1
Start with A0
Write A(t) = A0ekt and put in the starting amount. Decide what t = 0 means (a year, a moment).
2
Find k from a data point
Plug in a known (t, A). Divide by A0, take ln of both sides, divide by t.
3
Half-life or doubling
Half-life h: set A = ½A0 to get k = −ln 2 / h. Doubling: set A = 2A0 to get t = ln 2 / k.
4
Answer the question
“How much?” plug in t. “How long?” solve for t with ln, exactly as in Lesson 4.5.

💭 Notice: Keep k unrounded in your calculator until the final answer. Rounding k early can shift a prediction by hundreds.

Real-World Context

A medical isotope fades

A hospital isotope has a half-life of 8 days, so k = −ln 2 / 8 ≈ −0.0866. A patient receives a 120 mg dose, so A(t) = 120e−0.0866t. Every 8 days the amount is cut in half: 60 mg at day 8, 30 mg at day 16. How much is left after 20 days?

A(t) = 120ekt: half-life 8 days

Example: A(20) = 120e(−ln 2 / 8)(20) ≈ 21.21. To the nearest tenth, about 21.2 mg remains after 20 days.

Worked Example · Finding k From Data

A town had 24,000 people in 2020 and 27,500 in 2025. Predict 2032.

1
Set up
Let t = 0 be 2020: P(t) = 24,000ekt. In 2025, t = 5: 27,500 = 24,000e5k.
2
Solve for k
e5k = 27,500 / 24,000, so 5k = ln(27,500 / 24,000) and k ≈ 0.0272.
3
Predict
2032 is t = 12: P(12) = 24,000e12k ≈ 33,274. To the nearest hundred, about 33,300 people.

💭 Common slip: Measure t from the starting year. Plugging in t = 2032 instead of t = 12 gives an absurd number.

Worked Example · Half-Life · Carbon Dating

A bone has 35% of its original carbon-14. How old is it?

1
Find k
Carbon-14 has a half-life of about 5730 years, so k = −ln 2 / 5730 ≈ −0.000121.
2
Set up
35% remains, so 0.35A0 = A0ekt. Divide by A0: 0.35 = ekt.
3
Solve for t
ln 0.35 = kt, so t = ln 0.35 / k ≈ 8679. To the nearest hundred, the bone is about 8,700 years old.

💭 Notice: A0 cancels, so you never need the original amount. Only the fraction left matters.

Worked Example · Doubling Time

$3,000 earns 5.2% compounded continuously. When does it double?

1
Set up
A = Pert with r = 0.052. Doubling means A = 6,000: 6,000 = 3,000e0.052t.
2
Simplify
2 = e0.052t, so ln 2 = 0.052t
3
Solve
t = ln 2 / 0.052 ≈ 13.33. To the nearest hundredth, it doubles in about 13.33 years.
A(t) = 3e0.052t (thousands of dollars)

💭 Notice: The $3,000 cancels: any amount at 5.2% doubles in the same 13.33 years. Doubling time depends only on the rate.

Worked Example · Newton’s Law of Cooling

Soup at 190°F cools to 150°F in 10 minutes in a 70°F room

Newton’s law of cooling: f(t) = T0 + Ce−kt, where f(t) is the temperature after t minutes, T0 is the temperature of the surroundings, and C and k are constants. Predict the temperature after 25 minutes, and find when the soup reaches 100°F.

1
Find C from the start
T0 = 70. At t = 0, f(0) = 70 + Ce0 = 70 + C = 190, so C = 120 and f(t) = 70 + 120e−kt.
2
Find k from the data point
f(10) = 150: 150 = 70 + 120e−10k, so e−10k = 80/120 = 2/3. Take ln: −10k = ln(2/3), so k = −ln(2/3)/10 ≈ 0.0405. Keep k unrounded in your calculator.
3
Predict the temperature
f(25) = 70 + 120e−25k ≈ 70 + 120(0.3629) ≈ 113.5. After 25 minutes the soup is about 114°F.
4
Find the time
Set 100 = 70 + 120e−kt: e−kt = 30/120 = 1/4, so t = ln 4 / k ≈ 34.19. The soup reaches 100°F after about 34.2 minutes.
f(t) = 70 + 120e−kt: the soup approaches the 70°F room temperature

💭 Common slip: Subtract the room temperature before you take ln. Setting 100 = 120e−kt leaves out T0 and gives the wrong time.

Your Turn — Guided Practice

Now you try, with me right here

Work each one, then tap your answer.

Question 1 — A substance has a half-life of 12 years. Find k to the nearest ten-thousandth.

Here's why: k = −ln 2 / 12 ≈ −0.6931 / 12 ≈ −0.0578. −0.0251 uses log 2 instead of ln 2. The model uses base e, so the log has to be ln.

Question 2 — A bacteria culture grows by P(t) = P0e0.35t, t in hours. Find the doubling time to the nearest hundredth.

Here's why: Set 2 = e0.35t: t = ln 2 / 0.35 ≈ 1.98 hours. 5.71 is 2 / 0.35, which forgets to take ln of 2.

Question 3: Coffee at 180°F is set in a 70°F room. In f(t) = T0 + Ce−kt, what is C?

Here's why: T0 is the room temperature, 70. At t = 0, f(0) = 70 + C = 180, so C = 110, the starting difference between the coffee and the room. C = 180 confuses C with the starting temperature of the coffee.

Why This Matters

One model, a hundred uses

Population, money, medicine, radioactivity, and temperature all change in proportion to how much there is. Once you can find k and solve for t, you can read and check the claims people make about all of them.

This shows up everywhere

  • Carbon dating in archaeology and drug half-lives in pharmacy both use k = −ln 2 / h.
  • Newton’s law of cooling, f(t) = T0 + Ce−kt, is the same model shifted up by the temperature of the surroundings.
  • Continuous compounding from Lesson 4.2 is this model with k = r, the interest rate.

Mini Knowledge Test

Show what you know — 7 quick questions

Question 1
A sample decays by A(t) = 500e−0.04t grams, t in years. How much remains after 10 years, to the nearest tenth?
Here's why: A(10) = 500e−0.4 ≈ 335.2 g. 745.9 drops the negative sign, 300.0 treats 4% as simple loss (500 × 0.6), and 480.4 forgets to multiply by t = 10.
Question 2
A substance has a half-life of 30 years. Find k to the nearest ten-thousandth.
Here's why: k = −ln 2 / 30 ≈ −0.0231. Positive 0.0231 would mean growth, −0.0100 uses log 2 instead of ln 2, and −0.0333 is just −1/30.
Question 3
A population grows from 8,000 to 10,000 in 4 years. Find k in P(t) = 8,000ekt, to the nearest ten-thousandth.
Here's why: e4k = 10,000 / 8,000 = 1.25, so k = ln 1.25 / 4 ≈ 0.0558. 0.0625 is the simple percent 25% ÷ 4, 0.0242 uses log instead of ln, and 0.2231 forgets to divide by 4.
Question 4
Money earns 6% compounded continuously. Find the doubling time to the nearest hundredth.
Here's why: t = ln 2 / 0.06 ≈ 11.55. 33.33 is 2 / 0.06 without ln, 5.02 uses log 2, and 12.00 is the rule-of-72 estimate, not the exact answer.
Question 5
Carbon-14 has a half-life of 5730 years. How long until only 20% of the original amount remains? Round to the nearest hundred years.
Here's why: k = −ln 2 / 5730, and 0.20 = ekt gives t = ln 0.2 / k ≈ 13,305, or about 13,300 years. 1,800 solves for 80% remaining instead of 20%, 2.3 is the number of half-lives without multiplying by 5730, and 4,600 is 0.8 × 5730.
Question 6
A pie at 350°F is set in a 75°F kitchen. After 20 minutes it is 250°F. Use f(t) = 75 + Ce−kt to find its temperature after 45 minutes, to the nearest degree.
Here's why: C = 350 − 75 = 275. From 250 = 75 + 275e−20k, e−20k = 175/275, so k = −ln(175/275)/20 ≈ 0.0226. Then f(45) = 75 + 275e−45k ≈ 75 + 99.5 ≈ 174°F. 164°F ignores the kitchen temperature, 125°F assumes the pie loses the same 5°F every minute, and 99°F forgets to add the 75 back.
Question 7
For the same pie, f(t) = 75 + 275e−kt with k = −ln(175/275)/20. How many minutes until it cools to 120°F? Round to the nearest tenth.
Here's why: Set 120 = 75 + 275e−kt: e−kt = 45/275, so t = ln(275/45)/k ≈ 1.8101 / 0.0226 ≈ 80.1 minutes. 36.7 sets 120 = 275e−kt without subtracting the 75. 63.6 ignores the kitchen temperature entirely, and 46.0 assumes a steady 5°F drop per minute.
