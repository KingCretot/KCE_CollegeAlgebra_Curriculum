Key Ideas · Lessons 4.1–4.2

Inverses and exponential functions

Lesson 4.1

Inverse functions

A function has an inverse only if it is one-to-one (passes the horizontal line test). To find f−1, swap x and y and solve for y; restrict the domain if needed. Check with f(f−1(x)) = x. The graphs reflect over y = x.

Example: f(x) = 2x + 6 → f−1(x) = (x − 6)/2
Lesson 4.2

Exponential functions

f(x) = ax grows if a > 1 and decays if 0 < a < 1, with asymptote y = 0 and range (0, ∞). Solve by matching bases. Compound interest A = P(1 + r/n)nt; continuous A = Pert.

Example: 9x = 27 → 32x = 33 → x = 3/2

Key Ideas · Lessons 4.3–4.4

Logarithms and how to evaluate them

Lesson 4.3

Logarithmic functions

y = loga x means ay = x. The graph reflects ax over y = x: domain (0, ∞), asymptote x = 0. Product, quotient, and power rules expand and condense logs.

Example: log2(8x³) = 3 + 3 log2 x
Lesson 4.4

Common logs, natural logs, change of base

log x is base 10 and ln x is base e. loga x = ln x / ln a. pH = −log[H3O+]. For a log function’s domain, set the argument > 0.

Example: log5 80 = ln 80 / ln 5 ≈ 2.72

Key Ideas · Lessons 4.5–4.6

Solving equations and building models

Lesson 4.5

Exponential and logarithmic equations

Exponential: isolate, take ln of both sides, bring the exponent down. Log: condense to one log, convert to exponential form, and reject extraneous answers that make an argument ≤ 0.

Example: log2 x + log2(x − 6) = 4 → x = 8 (reject −2)
Lesson 4.6

Growth and decay

A(t) = A0ekt. Find k from data with k = ln(A/A0)/t. Half-life h: k = −ln 2 / h. Doubling time: t = ln 2 / k.

Example: 5.2% continuous: doubles in ln 2 / 0.052 ≈ 13.33 years

Watch Out

The mistakes that cost the most points

The mistakeThe fix
Reading f−1(x) as 1/f(x)The −1 means inverse, not reciprocal. Swap x and y and solve.
Finding an inverse for a function that is not one-to-oneUse the horizontal line test first. For y = x², restrict to x ≥ 0.
Using simple interest in a compound interest problemCompound: A = P(1 + r/n)nt. Continuous: A = Pert.
Writing log(x + y) = log x + log yThe product rule is log(xy) = log x + log y. There is no rule for log of a sum.
Mixing up loga x = y with xy = aThe base stays the base: loga x = y means ay = x.
Dividing upside down in change of baseThe base goes in the denominator: log3 40 = ln 40 / ln 3.
Including the endpoint in a log domainThe argument must be > 0, never = 0. log(x − 7) has domain (7, ∞).
Keeping extraneous solutionsCheck every answer in the original log equation; reject any that makes an argument ≤ 0.
Using log instead of ln with base ee0.3x = 12 needs ln: x = ln 12 / 0.3.
Rounding k too earlyKeep k unrounded in the calculator and round only the final answer.

Practice Test

10 questions, every lesson mixed together

Just like a unit test, these jump between lessons. You get two tries on each question, and every question explains itself after you answer.

Question 1
Find the pH of a solution with [H3O+] = 8.1 × 10−6, to the nearest tenth.
Here's why: pH = −log(8.1 × 10−6) ≈ 5.09, or 5.1. −5.1 forgets the negative in the formula, 0.9 is log 8.1 alone, and 11.7 uses ln. (Lesson 4.4)
Question 2
Find f−1(x) for f(x) = 3x − 5.
Here's why: Swap: x = 3y − 5. Add 5: x + 5 = 3y. Divide: y = (x + 5)/3. 1/(3x − 5) is the reciprocal, not the inverse, and x/3 + 5 undoes the steps in the wrong order. (Lesson 4.1)
Question 3
Solve log x + log(x − 15) = 2.
Here's why: log[x(x − 15)] = 2, so x² − 15x = 100 and (x − 20)(x + 5) = 0. x = −5 makes log x undefined, so only x = 20 works. 15.13 comes from setting x(x − 15) = 2 instead of 10². (Lesson 4.5)
Question 4
Solve 8x = 32.
Here's why: Write both as powers of 2: 23x = 25, so 3x = 5 and x = 5/3. 4 is 32 ÷ 8, 3/5 is upside down, and 5/2 writes 8 as 2². (Lesson 4.2)
Question 5
A drug has a half-life of 6 hours. A patient takes 80 mg. How much remains after 15 hours, to the nearest tenth?
Here's why: k = −ln 2 / 6, so A(15) = 80e15k = 80(½)2.5 ≈ 14.1 mg. 60.6 flips the ratio to 6/15, 20.0 counts only two full half-lives, and 40.0 halves just once. (Lesson 4.6)
Question 6
Write log3(x²√y / z) as a sum and difference of logs. Assume x, y, z > 0.
Here's why: Product rule for the top, quotient rule for z, power rule for x² and √y = y1/2. Dividing by z subtracts its log; the power rule puts 2 in front, not as an exponent on the log; and a product inside becomes a sum, not a product of logs. (Lesson 4.3)
Question 7
Solve 7x + 1 = 30 to the nearest hundredth.
Here's why: (x + 1) ln 7 = ln 30, so x = ln 30 / ln 7 − 1 ≈ 1.7479 − 1 = 0.75. 2.75 adds 1 instead of subtracting, 3.29 is 30/7 − 1, and 0.48 is log 30 − 1, which skips dividing by log 7. (Lesson 4.5)
Question 8
$4,000 is invested at 3% compounded monthly. Find the amount after 5 years.
Here's why: A = 4000(1 + 0.03/12)60 ≈ $4,646.47. $4,600.00 is simple interest, $4,637.10 compounds annually, and $4,647.34 compounds continuously. (Lesson 4.2)
Question 9
Evaluate log6 100 to the nearest hundredth.
Here's why: log6 100 = ln 100 / ln 6 ≈ 4.6052 / 1.7918 ≈ 2.57. 0.39 divides upside down, 16.67 is 100 ÷ 6, and 1.22 is log(100/6). (Lesson 4.4)
Question 10
Solve log4 x = 3/2.
Here's why: Convert: x = 43/2 = (√4)³ = 8. 6 multiplies 4 by 3/2, 81/16 is (3/2)4 with base and exponent swapped, and 2.52 is 42/3. (Lesson 4.3)
