Let's Begin

When the graphs are curves

In a nonlinear system, at least one equation is not a line: a parabola, a circle, or another curve. Curves can cross more than once, so these systems often have two, three, or four solutions.

Today you'll solve nonlinear systems by substitution and elimination and use graphs to predict how many solutions to expect.

By the end of today, you will be able to:

  • 1Solve a nonlinear system by substitution
  • 2Solve a nonlinear system by elimination
  • 3Predict and check the number of solutions with a graph

Vocabulary

The words for this lesson

Nonlinear System

A system in which at least one equation is not linear, such as x2 + y2 = 25.

💡Example: y = x2 and y = 4 is a nonlinear system.
Substitution

Solve one equation for a variable (often the linear one) and substitute into the other.

💡Best when: One equation is linear, or one variable is already isolated.
Elimination

Add or subtract equations to remove a variable or a squared term, like x2.

💡Best when: Both equations have x2 and y2 terms lined up.
Number of Solutions

Count the intersection points. A line and a circle meet 0, 1, or 2 times; a circle and a hyperbola can meet up to 4 times.

💡Memory hook: Each intersection point is one solution.

How It Works

Solving a nonlinear system

1
Picture it
Identify each graph: line, parabola, circle, or hyperbola. Think about how many times they could cross.
2
Choose a method
Substitution if one equation is linear; elimination if both have matching squared terms.
3
Solve the one-variable equation
You often get a quadratic. Factor it or use the quadratic formula.
4
Find every partner and check
Each x-value can give one or more y-values. Check every pair in both original equations.

💭 Notice: When elimination gives x2 = 9, remember both x = 3 and x = −3. Missing the negative root loses half the solutions.

Real-World Context

Can the drone reach the signal?

A drone flies along the line y = x + 1 (in miles). A cell tower at the origin has a range of 5 miles, so its coverage edge is the circle x2 + y2 = 25. Where does the drone enter and leave coverage?

Example: Solving the system gives (−4, −3) and (3, 4). The drone is in range between those two points and out of range everywhere else on its path.

Worked Example · Substitution

Solve x2 + y2 = 25 and y = x + 1

1
Substitute
x2 + (x + 1)2 = 25, so 2x2 + 2x + 1 = 25.
2
Solve the quadratic
2x2 + 2x − 24 = 0, so x2 + x − 12 = 0 and (x + 4)(x − 3) = 0. x = −4 or x = 3.
3
Find y
y = x + 1 gives y = −3 and y = 4. The solutions are (3, 4) and (−4, −3).
x2 + y2 = 25 and y = x + 1 meet at (3, 4) and (−4, −3)

💭 Common slip: (x + 1)2 is x2 + 2x + 1, not x2 + 1. Writing out the middle term is the step most often skipped.

Worked Example · Elimination

Solve x2 + y2 = 13 and x2 − y2 = 5

1
Add the equations
2x2 = 18, so x2 = 9 and x = ±3.
2
Find y
9 + y2 = 13, so y2 = 4 and y = ±2.
3
List every pair
(3, 2), (3, −2), (−3, 2), (−3, −2), four solutions.
x2 + y2 = 13 and x2 − y2 = 5 meet at four points

💭 Common slip: Each x pairs with both y-values here because x and y appear only as squares. Check that every combination works.

Worked Example · Parabola and Line

Solve y = x2 − 4 and y = 2x − 1

1
Set the expressions equal
x2 − 4 = 2x − 1, so x2 − 2x − 3 = 0.
2
Factor
(x − 3)(x + 1) = 0, so x = 3 or x = −1.
3
Find y from the line
y = 2(3) − 1 = 5 and y = 2(−1) − 1 = −3. The solutions are (3, 5) and (−1, −3).
y = x2 − 4 and y = 2x − 1 meet at (−1, −3) and (3, 5)

💭 Common slip: Use the simpler equation, usually the linear one, to find y. It gives fewer chances for an arithmetic slip.

Your Turn — Guided Practice

Now you try, with me right here

Work each one, then tap your answer.

Question 1 — How many times can a line and a circle intersect?

Here's why: A line crosses a circle at most twice. It can also just touch it once or miss it entirely. Up to 4 is the limit for two conic sections like a circle and a hyperbola.

Question 2 — Solve y = x2 and y = 4.

Here's why: x2 = 4 has two solutions, x = 2 and x = −2, and both give y = 4.

Why This Matters

Curves cross in the real world

Orbits, ranges, profit curves, and projectile paths are rarely straight lines. Finding where two curves meet answers questions like “when are we in range?” or “where does the ball land on the slope?”

Mini Knowledge Test

Show what you know — 5 quick questions

Question 1
Solve y = x2 and y = x + 2.
Here's why: x2 = x + 2 gives x2 − x − 2 = 0, so (x − 2)(x + 1) = 0. x = 2 gives y = 4 and x = −1 gives y = 1. (−2, 4) and (1, 1) use the wrong signs, and (4, 2) and (1, −1) swap x and y.
Question 2
Solve x2 + y2 = 10 and y = 3x.
Here's why: x2 + 9x2 = 10, so x2 = 1 and x = ±1. Then y = 3x gives 3 and −3. (−1, 3) is not on the line y = 3x, and (3, 1) swaps the coordinates.
Question 3
How many real solutions does x2 + y2 = 4 and y = 5 have?
Here's why: The circle has radius 2, so its highest point is y = 2. The line y = 5 is above it and never touches it. Algebraically, x2 = 4 − 25 = −21 has no real solution.
Question 4
Solve xy = 6 and x + y = 5.
Here's why: y = 5 − x gives x(5 − x) = 6, so x2 − 5x + 6 = 0 and x = 2 or 3. (1, 6) has the right product but sums to 7, and the negatives sum to −5.
Question 5
Which point solves both x2 + y2 = 20 and x2 − y2 = 12?
Here's why: Adding gives 2x2 = 32, so x = ±4, and y2 = 4, so y = ±2. (2, 4) satisfies the first equation but gives 4 − 16 = −12 in the second. (16, 4) uses x2 as x.
