Let's Begin

Doing arithmetic with whole tables of numbers

A matrix can hold a whole table of data: hours by subject, prices by store, points in a picture. Matrix operations let you work with the whole table at once.

Today you'll add, subtract, and multiply matrices by a number, multiply two matrices, and decide when a product is defined.

By the end of today, you will be able to:

  • 1Add, subtract, and find scalar multiples of matrices
  • 2Multiply two matrices and find the dimensions of the product
  • 3Recognize that matrix multiplication is not commutative

Vocabulary

The words for this lesson

Equal Matrices

Same dimensions and every corresponding entry equal.

💡Example: [x 2] = [5 y] means x = 5 and y = 2.
Sum & Difference

Add or subtract corresponding entries. Only matrices with the same dimensions can be added.

💡Example: [1 2] + [3 −4] = [4 −2]
Scalar Multiple

Multiply every entry by the number k.

💡Example: 3[1 −2] = [3 −6]
Matrix Product

For AB, the entry in row i, column j is row i of A times column j of B: multiply matching entries and add. A must have as many columns as B has rows.

💡Dimensions: (m × n)(n × p) gives m × p. The inside numbers must match.

Notation: to save space, a matrix is sometimes written on one line with its rows separated by semicolons. [1 2; 3 4] means the matrix with first row 1, 2 and second row 3, 4.

How It Works

Multiplying two matrices

1
Check the dimensions
Write the dimensions side by side, like (2 × 3)(3 × 2). The inside numbers must match; the outside numbers give the size of the answer.
2
Row times column
For each entry, take a row of A and a column of B. Multiply the first entries, the second entries, and so on, then add.
3
Fill in every entry
Repeat for every row of A with every column of B.
4
Do not switch the order
AB and BA are usually different, and one may not even be defined.

💭 Notice: Matrix multiplication is not multiplying matching entries. [1 2; 3 4] times [5 0; −1 2] is not [5 0; −3 8].

Real-World Context

Total cost of tutoring

Two students book tutoring each week. Student 1 has 3 hours of math and 2 of reading; student 2 has 1 hour of math and 4 of reading. Math costs $40 an hour and reading $35.

Example: Hours matrix [3 2; 1 4] times price column [40; 35] gives [3(40) + 2(35); 1(40) + 4(35)] = [190; 180]: $190 for student 1 and $180 for student 2.

Worked Example · Combining

For A = [2 −1; 0 3] and B = [4 1; −2 5], find 2A − B

1
Scalar multiple
2A = [4 −2; 0 6]
2
Subtract entry by entry
4 − 4 = 0, −2 − 1 = −3, 0 − (−2) = 2, 6 − 5 = 1
3
Write the matrix
2A − B = [0 −3; 2 1]

0−3
21

💭 Common slip: Subtracting a negative entry adds: 0 − (−2) = 2.

Worked Example · Multiplying

Find [1 2; 3 4] · [5 0; −1 2]

1
Row 1
Row 1 times column 1: 1(5) + 2(−1) = 3. Row 1 times column 2: 1(0) + 2(2) = 4.
2
Row 2
Row 2 times column 1: 3(5) + 4(−1) = 11. Row 2 times column 2: 3(0) + 4(2) = 8.
3
Write the product
[3 4; 11 8]

The product:

34
118

💭 Common slip: Each entry uses a whole row and a whole column. That is why the row length of A must match the column length of B.

Worked Example · Dimensions

A = [1 0 2; −1 3 1] is 2 × 3 and B = [3 1; 2 1; 1 0] is 3 × 2. Find AB and the size of BA.

1
AB
(2 × 3)(3 × 2): the inside 3s match, so AB is 2 × 2.
2
Compute
Row 1: 3 + 0 + 2 = 5 and 1 + 0 + 0 = 1. Row 2: −3 + 6 + 1 = 4 and −1 + 3 + 0 = 2. AB = [5 1; 4 2].
3
BA
(3 × 2)(2 × 3) is defined too, but it is 3 × 3. AB and BA cannot be equal; they are not even the same size.

AB =

51
42

💭 Common slip: Never assume AB = BA. Even for two square matrices of the same size, the products are usually different.

Work each one, then tap your answer.

Question 1 — Can you add a 2 × 3 matrix and a 3 × 2 matrix?

Here's why: Addition needs the same dimensions, entry for entry. A 2 × 3 and a 3 × 2 matrix do not match.

Question 2 — Find 3 · [1 −2; 0 4].

Here's why: A scalar multiplies every entry. [3 −2; 0 4] multiplies only the first entry.

Why This Matters

Matrices run computer graphics and data

Every time a video game turns, shrinks, or moves a picture, it multiplies the coordinates by a matrix. Spreadsheets, search engines, and machine learning all depend on matrix products.

This shows up everywhere

Mini Knowledge Test

Show what you know — 5 quick questions

Question 1
A is 3 × 4 and B is 4 × 2. What are the dimensions of AB?
Here's why: The inside numbers (4 and 4) match, so AB is defined, and the outside numbers give 3 × 2.
Question 2
A = [1 3; 2 −1] and B = [4 0; 1 5]. What is the entry in row 2, column 1 of AB?
Here's why: Row 2 of A times column 1 of B: 2(4) + (−1)(1) = 8 − 1 = 7. 8 stops after the first product, and 9 adds the 1 instead of subtracting it.
Question 3
Which statement is true for matrices in general?
Here's why: Matrix multiplication is not commutative, but addition is. Two 2 × 3 matrices can be added, yet their product is not defined.
Question 4
A = [1 4] and B = [2 −1]. Find 2A − 3B.
Here's why: 2A = [2 8] and 3B = [6 −3]. Then [2 − 6, 8 − (−3)] = [−4 11]. [−4 5] treats −(−3) as −3, and [−1 5] is A − B.
Question 5
A is 2 × 3. Which product is defined?
Here's why: AB needs B to have 3 rows, so a 3 × 4 matrix works. For BA with B 3 × 3, B has 3 columns but A has only 2 rows.
