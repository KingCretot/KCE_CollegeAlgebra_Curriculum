Key Ideas · R.1 – R.3

Sets, real numbers, and polynomials

Lesson R.1

Sets

A set is a collection of elements. A ∪ B holds everything in either set, A ∩ B only what is in both, and A′ everything in U that is not in A. A set with n elements has 2n subsets.

Example: If A = {1, 2, 3} and B = {2, 3, 4}, A ∩ B = {2, 3} and A ∪ B = {1, 2, 3, 4}.
Lesson R.2

Real numbers and order of operations

Classify numbers as natural, whole, integer, rational, or irrational. Evaluate parentheses, exponents, multiplication and division, then addition and subtraction. Distance on a number line is |b − a|.

Example: −32 = −9, but (−3)2 = 9.
Lesson R.3

Polynomials

Add exponents to multiply like bases and multiply exponents for a power of a power. Multiply polynomials by distributing every term, and divide with long division.

Example: (x + 5)2 = x2 + 10x + 25

Key Ideas · R.4 – R.5

Factoring and rational expressions

Lesson R.4

Factoring

Take out the GCF first. Then count terms: two terms means squares or cubes, three means a trinomial, four means grouping. Factor completely.

Example: x3 − 8 = (x − 2)(x2 + 2x + 4)
Lesson R.5

Rational expressions

Factor the top and bottom, find the domain from the original denominator, then divide out common factors. Add and subtract with the LCD; simplify complex fractions by multiplying by the LCD.

Example: (x2 − 1)/(x + 1) = x − 1, with x ≠ −1

Key Ideas · R.6 – R.7

Rational exponents and radicals

Lesson R.6

Rational exponents

a−n = 1/an and am/n = (n√a)m. All the exponent rules still apply. Factor out the smallest power.

Example: 8−2/3 = 1/(81/3)2 = 1/4
Lesson R.7

Radicals

Pull perfect powers out of the radicand, combine like radicals, and rationalize denominators, using the conjugate for a binomial.

Example: √12 + √27 = 2√3 + 3√3 = 5√3

Watch Out

The mistakes that cost the most points

The mistakeThe fix
Writing −32 = 9The exponent belongs only to the 3: −32 = −9. Use (−3)2 for 9.
Writing x3 · x4 = x12Multiplying like bases adds exponents: x7. Multiply exponents only for a power of a power.
Writing (x + 5)2 = x2 + 25Square a binomial with the pattern: x2 + 10x + 25.
Dropping the minus sign when subtracting a polynomialChange the sign of every term in the polynomial being subtracted.
Stopping before factoring completelyCheck every factor again. x2 − 4 inside an answer still factors.
Trying to factor x2 + 9A sum of squares does not factor over the real numbers.
Canceling terms instead of factorsFactor first. In (x + 6)/6, the 6s are not common factors.
Finding the domain after cancelingFind the domain from the original denominator, before anything divides out.
Reading 2−3 as −8A negative exponent means a reciprocal: 2−3 = 1/8.
Writing √(a + b) = √a + √bThere is no sum rule for radicals: √(9 + 16) = 5, not 7.

Practice Test

10 questions, every lesson mixed together

Just like a unit test, these jump between lessons. You get two tries on each question, and every question explains itself after you answer.

Question 1
Let U = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}, A = {1, 2, 3}, and B = {2, 4, 6}. Find A′ ∪ B.
Here's why: A′ = {4, 5, 6}. Its union with {2, 4, 6} is {2, 4, 5, 6}. {4, 6} is A′ ∩ B, {2} is A ∩ B, and {1, 2, 3, 4, 6} is A ∪ B. (Lesson R.1)
Question 2
Evaluate −24 + (−2)3.
Here's why: −24 = −16 and (−2)3 = −8, so the sum is −24. 8 reads −24 as +16, 24 makes both terms positive, and −8 drops the first term. (Lesson R.2)
Question 3
Multiply (2x − 5)2.
Here's why: (2x)2 − 2(2x)(5) + 52 = 4x2 − 20x + 25. 4x2 + 25 skips the middle term, 4x2 − 10x + 25 forgets to double it, and 2x2 − 20x + 25 does not square the 2. (Lesson R.3)
Question 4
Factor completely: 3x3 − 75x
Here's why: The GCF is 3x, leaving x2 − 25 = (x + 5)(x − 5). 3x(x2 − 25) and 3(x3 − 25x) stop early, and 3x(x − 5)2 has a middle term. (Lesson R.4)
Question 5
Factor 3x2 − 10x − 8.
Here's why: ac = −24, and 2 and −12 multiply to −24 and add to −10. Multiplying out the other choices gives middle terms of +10x, +2x, and −2x. (Lesson R.4)
Question 6
Write (x2 − 25)/(x2 + 2x − 15) in lowest terms.
Here's why: Top: (x − 5)(x + 5). Bottom: (x + 5)(x − 3). Divide out x + 5. (x + 5)/(x − 3) divides out the wrong factor, and 5/3 and −25/(2x − 15) cancel terms instead of factors. (Lesson R.5)
Question 7
Add: 2/x + 3/(x + 1)
Here's why: 2(x + 1) + 3x = 5x + 2 over the LCD x(x + 1). 5/(2x + 1) adds tops and bottoms, 5/(x(x + 1)) adds only the numerators, and 5x + 3 multiplies 3 by x + 1 instead of x. (Lesson R.5)
Question 8
Evaluate 8−2/3.
Here's why: 81/3 = 2, 22 = 4, and the negative exponent gives 1/4. A negative exponent never makes the answer negative, and −16/3 multiplies 8 by −2/3. (Lesson R.6)
Question 9
Simplify (x3/4)2 · x−1/2.
Here's why: (x3/4)2 = x3/2, and x3/2 · x−1/2 = x1 = x. x1/4 forgets the square (3/4 − 1/2), x5/4 forgets the square and adds the 1/2, and x3/2 ignores the second factor. (Lesson R.6)
Question 10
Rationalize the denominator of 6/(√7 + 1).
Here's why: Multiply by the conjugate √7 − 1: the bottom becomes 7 − 1 = 6, and 6(√7 − 1)/6 = √7 − 1. 6√7 − 6 forgets to divide by 6, (√7 − 1)/6 divides by 6 twice, and √7 + 1 keeps the wrong sign. (Lesson R.7)
