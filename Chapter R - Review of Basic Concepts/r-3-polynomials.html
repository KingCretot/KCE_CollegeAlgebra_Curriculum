Let's Begin

The expressions algebra is built from

A polynomial is a sum of terms like 4x3, −2x, and 7. Profit, area, and the path of a ball are all polynomials. Most of this course is about working with them.

Today you'll use the exponent rules, then add, subtract, multiply, and divide polynomials, including the special products you should recognize on sight.

By the end of today, you will be able to:

  • 1Use the product, power, and zero-exponent rules
  • 2Add, subtract, and multiply polynomials, including special products
  • 3Divide a polynomial by a monomial and by a binomial

Vocabulary

The words for this lesson

Product Rule

am · an = am + n. Same base: add the exponents.

💡Example: x4 · x3 = x7, not x12.
Power Rules

(am)n = amn, (ab)m = ambm, and a0 = 1 for a ≠ 0.

💡Example: (2x3)4 = 16x12
Degree

The degree of a term is the sum of its exponents. The degree of a polynomial is the greatest degree of any of its terms.

💡Example: 5x3 − 2x + 9 has degree 3.
Special Products

(x + y)2 = x2 + 2xy + y2 and (x + y)(x − y) = x2 − y2.

💡Common slip: (x + 5)2 is x2 + 10x + 25, not x2 + 25.

How It Works

Working with polynomials

1
Add or subtract
Combine like terms: same variable, same exponent. To subtract, change the sign of every term in the second polynomial.
2
Multiply
Multiply every term in the first by every term in the second, then combine like terms. For two binomials, FOIL is a checklist for this.
3
Divide by a monomial
Divide each term of the top by the monomial: (6x3 + 9x)/3x = 2x2 + 3.
4
Divide by a polynomial
Use long division: divide, multiply, subtract, bring down, and repeat until the remainder has lower degree than the divisor.

💭 Notice: When you subtract (5x2 − 3x) − (2x2 − 7x), the minus sign reaches both terms: 5x2 − 3x − 2x2 + 7x = 3x2 + 4x.

Real-World Context

Area of a garden with a border

A square garden is x feet on a side. A path 3 feet wide runs all the way around it, so the garden plus path is a square (x + 6) feet on a side.

Example: Total area = (x + 6)2 = x2 + 12x + 36 square feet. The path alone is that minus the garden: x2 + 12x + 36 − x2 = 12x + 36 square feet. For a 10-foot garden, that is 156 square feet of path.

Worked Example · Exponent Rules

Simplify (−3x2y)3(2xy4)

1
Power rule
(−3x2y)3 = (−3)3x6y3 = −27x6y3
2
Multiply the coefficients
−27 · 2 = −54
3
Product rule on each variable
x6 · x = x7 and y3 · y4 = y7, so the answer is −54x7y7

💭 Common slip: The power applies to the coefficient too. (−3)3 = −27, not −9 and not −3.

Worked Example · Multiplying

Multiply (2x − 3)(x2 + 4x − 5)

1
Distribute the first term
2x · x2 + 2x · 4x + 2x · (−5) = 2x3 + 8x2 − 10x
2
Distribute the second term
−3x2 − 12x + 15
3
Combine like terms
2x3 + 5x2 − 22x + 15

💭 Common slip: FOIL only works for two binomials. When one factor has three terms, distribute every term; there are 2 × 3 = 6 products before combining.

Worked Example · Long Division

Divide (3x3 − 2x2 + 5x − 1) by (x − 1)

1
First term
3x3 ÷ x = 3x2. Multiply: 3x3 − 3x2. Subtract: x2 + 5x.
2
Next terms
x2 ÷ x = x. Multiply: x2 − x. Subtract: 6x − 1. Then 6x ÷ x = 6. Multiply: 6x − 6. Subtract: remainder 5.
3
Write the answer
3x2 + x + 6 + 5/(x − 1). Check: (x − 1)(3x2 + x + 6) + 5 gives back the original.

💭 Common slip: Subtracting a whole row is where errors happen. Change the signs of the row you are subtracting, then add.

Your Turn — Guided Practice

Now you try, with me right here

Work each one, then tap your answer.

Question 1 — Simplify (x3)4.

Here's why: A power of a power multiplies the exponents: 3 × 4 = 12. x7 adds them, which is the rule for x3 · x4.

Question 2 — Multiply (x − 6)2.

Here's why: (x − 6)(x − 6) = x2 − 6x − 6x + 36 = x2 − 12x + 36. x2 + 36 skips the middle term and the sign of the constant.

Why This Matters

Polynomials are the main character

Chapter 3 is entirely about polynomial functions, and factoring (next lesson) is multiplying in reverse. Getting these products fast and right saves time on every problem that follows.

Mini Knowledge Test

Show what you know — 5 quick questions

Question 1
Simplify (4a2b)(−2ab3)2.
Here's why: (−2ab3)2 = 4a2b6. Then 4 · 4 = 16, a2 · a2 = a4, b · b6 = b7. Squaring makes −2 positive, −8 forgets to square the coefficient, and 16a4b6 multiplies the b exponents (1 · 6) instead of adding them.
Question 2
Subtract: (6x2 − 4x + 1) − (2x2 + 3x − 5).
Here's why: Change every sign in the second polynomial and add: 6x2 − 4x + 1 − 2x2 − 3x + 5 = 4x2 − 7x + 6. The other choices keep at least one of the original signs.
Question 3
Multiply (3x + 2)(3x − 2).
Here's why: This is a sum times a difference: (3x)2 − 22 = 9x2 − 4. The middle terms +6x and −6x cancel. 6x2 doubles 3x instead of squaring it.
Question 4
What is the degree of 4x2y3 − 7xy + 2?
Here's why: The degree of a term adds its exponents: x2y3 has degree 2 + 3 = 5, the highest. 2 and 3 look at only one variable, and 4 is a coefficient.
Question 5
Divide (x2 + 5x + 9) by (x + 2).
Here's why: x2 ÷ x = x; subtract x2 + 2x to get 3x + 9. 3x ÷ x = 3; subtract 3x + 6 to get remainder 3. x + 3 leaves off the remainder, x + 7 + 23/(x + 2) adds instead of subtracting, and x + 3 + 9/(x + 2) keeps the original 9 instead of subtracting the 6.
