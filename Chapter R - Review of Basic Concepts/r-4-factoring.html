Let's Begin

Multiplying, run in reverse

Factoring rewrites a sum as a product. It is the step that solves quadratic equations, simplifies fractions, and finds the zeros of a graph, so it shows up in almost every chapter that follows.

Today you'll pull out a greatest common factor, factor by grouping, factor trinomials, and spot the special patterns that factor on sight.

By the end of today, you will be able to:

  • 1Factor out the greatest common factor and factor by grouping
  • 2Factor trinomials, including perfect square trinomials
  • 3Factor differences of squares, sums and differences of cubes, and use substitution

Vocabulary

The words for this lesson

Greatest Common Factor

The largest expression that divides every term. Always look for it first.

💡Example: 12x3 + 18x2 = 6x2(2x + 3)
Factoring by Grouping

For four terms: group them in pairs, factor each pair, then factor out the shared binomial.

💡Example: x3 + 2x2 + 5x + 10 = x2(x + 2) + 5(x + 2) = (x + 2)(x2 + 5)
Difference of Squares

x2 − y2 = (x + y)(x − y).

💡Common slip: x2 + y2, a sum of squares, does not factor over the real numbers.
Sum & Difference of Cubes

x3 + y3 = (x + y)(x2 − xy + y2) and x3 − y3 = (x − y)(x2 + xy + y2).

💡Memory hook: SOAP gives the signs: Same, Opposite, Always Positive.

How It Works

A factoring checklist

1
GCF first
Factor out the greatest common factor before anything else. It makes every later step smaller.
2
Count the terms
Two terms: look for a difference of squares or a sum or difference of cubes. Three terms: a trinomial. Four terms: try grouping.
3
Trinomials
For ax2 + bx + c, find two numbers whose product is ac and whose sum is b. Split the middle term with them, then factor by grouping.
4
Check
Multiply your factors back out. You should get the original polynomial, and no factor should factor any further.

💭 Notice: “Factor completely” means keep going. x4 − 16 = (x2 + 4)(x2 − 4) is not finished; x2 − 4 still factors into (x + 2)(x − 2).

Real-World Context

Finding the dimensions of a rectangle

A rectangular patio has area x2 + 7x + 12 square feet. Factoring the area gives the length and width as expressions in x.

Example: x2 + 7x + 12 = (x + 3)(x + 4), so the patio is (x + 3) feet by (x + 4) feet. When x = 5, that is 8 ft by 9 ft, and 8 × 9 = 72 = 25 + 35 + 12.

Worked Example · Trinomials

Factor 6x2 − 7x − 20 completely

1
Find the key product
a · c = 6(−20) = −120. You need two numbers that multiply to −120 and add to −7: 8 and −15.
2
Split and group
6x2 + 8x − 15x − 20 = 2x(3x + 4) − 5(3x + 4)
3
Factor out the binomial
(3x + 4)(2x − 5). Check: 6x2 − 15x + 8x − 20 = 6x2 − 7x − 20.

💭 Common slip: Factor −5 (not 5) out of −15x − 20 so the two binomials match: −5(3x + 4).

Worked Example · Difference of Cubes

Factor 8x3 − 27

1
Write as cubes
8x3 = (2x)3 and 27 = 33, so x → 2x and y → 3.
2
Use the pattern
(x − y)(x2 + xy + y2) becomes (2x − 3)((2x)2 + (2x)(3) + 32)
3
Simplify
(2x − 3)(4x2 + 6x + 9). The trinomial does not factor further.

💭 Common slip: The middle term is +6x, not +12x. There is no 2 in the cube pattern; that 2 belongs to the square of a binomial.

Worked Example · Grouping

Factor x3 + 3x2 − 4x − 12 completely

1
Group in pairs
(x3 + 3x2) + (−4x − 12) = x2(x + 3) − 4(x + 3)
2
Factor out the binomial
(x + 3)(x2 − 4)
3
Keep going
x2 − 4 is a difference of squares: (x + 3)(x − 2)(x + 2)

💭 Common slip: Stopping at (x + 3)(x2 − 4) loses points on a “factor completely” question. Always check every factor one more time.

Worked Example · Factoring by Substitution

Factor 6(4z − 3)2 + 7(4z − 3) − 3 completely

1
Replace the repeated expression
Let u = 4z − 3. The expression becomes 6u2 + 7u − 3, an ordinary trinomial.
2
Factor the trinomial in u
a · c = 6(−3) = −18. Use 9 and −2: 6u2 + 9u − 2u − 3 = 3u(2u + 3) − 1(2u + 3) = (3u − 1)(2u + 3).
3
Put 4z − 3 back in and simplify
[3(4z − 3) − 1][2(4z − 3) + 3] = (12z − 10)(8z − 3). Factor 2 out of 12z − 10: 2(6z − 5)(8z − 3).

💭 Common slip: Do not stop at (3u − 1)(2u + 3). The answer must be in terms of z, and it is not factored completely until the common factor 2 comes out of 12z − 10.

Question 1: Factor x2 + 5x − 14.

Here's why: You need two numbers that multiply to −14 and add to +5: 7 and −2. (x − 7)(x + 2) multiplies to x2 − 5x − 14, the wrong middle sign.

Question 2: Factor 9y2 − 25.

Here's why: 9y2 − 25 = (3y)2 − 52, a difference of squares. (3y − 5)2 = 9y2 − 30y + 25 has a middle term.

Why This Matters

Factoring is how equations get solved

When a product equals zero, one of its factors must be zero. That one fact turns factoring into a way to solve equations, find x-intercepts, and simplify fractions.

This shows up everywhere

  • Lesson R.5 simplifies rational expressions by factoring the top and bottom.
  • Lesson 1.4 solves quadratic equations by factoring and the zero-product property.
  • Lesson 3.3 finds the zeros of polynomial functions from their factors.

Mini Knowledge Test

Show what you know: 5 quick questions

Question 1
Factor completely: 12x3y2 − 18x2y3
Here's why: The GCF of 12 and 18 is 6, and the lowest powers are x2 and y2. 2x2y2(6x − 9y) is not complete because 6x − 9y still has a 3 in common. The other two change the sign or leave an extra y.
Question 2
Factor 4x2 + 12x + 9.
Here's why: 4x2 = (2x)2, 9 = 32, and 2(2x)(3) = 12x, so it is a perfect square trinomial. (4x + 3)(x + 3) gives 15x, (2x − 3)2 gives −12x, and (2x + 9)(2x + 1) gives 20x.
Question 3
Factor x3 + 64.
Here's why: x3 + 43 is a sum of cubes: same sign, opposite sign, always positive. (x − 4)(x2 + 4x + 16) is x3 − 64, (x + 4)(x2 + 4x + 16) has the wrong middle sign, and (x + 4)3 has extra middle terms.
Question 4
Factor 2x2 + x − 15.
Here's why: ac = −30, and 6 and −5 multiply to −30 and add to 1. Multiplying out the other choices gives middle terms of −x, +7x, and −7x.
Question 5
Factor (x + 1)2 − 5(x + 1) + 6.
Here's why: Let u = x + 1. Then u2 − 5u + 6 = (u − 2)(u − 3) = (x + 1 − 2)(x + 1 − 3) = (x − 1)(x − 2). (x − 2)(x − 3) forgets to put x + 1 back in, and (x + 3)(x + 4) uses the wrong signs.
