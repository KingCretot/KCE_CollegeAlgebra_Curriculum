Let's Begin

Fractions, with polynomials on top and bottom

A rational expression is a fraction whose top and bottom are polynomials. Every rule you know for fractions still works. The new part is that the denominator can never be zero.

Today you'll find the domain of a rational expression, write it in lowest terms, and multiply, divide, add, subtract, and simplify complex fractions.

By the end of today, you will be able to:

  • 1Find the domain of a rational expression and write it in lowest terms
  • 2Multiply, divide, add, and subtract rational expressions
  • 3Simplify complex fractions

Vocabulary

The words for this lesson

Rational Expression

A quotient P/Q of two polynomials, with Q ≠ 0.

💡Example: (x + 2)/(x2 − 9)
Domain

All real numbers except the values that make the denominator 0.

💡Example: For (x + 2)/(x2 − 9), x ≠ 3 and x ≠ −3.
Lowest Terms

Factor the top and bottom completely, then divide out common factors.

💡Common slip: (x + 6)/6 is not x. The 6 is a term on top, not a factor.
Least Common Denominator

The product of every factor that appears in any denominator, each to its highest power.

💡Example: For 1/(x − 1) and 1/(x − 1)2, the LCD is (x − 1)2.

How It Works

Working with rational expressions

1
Factor everything
Factor every numerator and denominator before you do anything else.
2
Multiply and divide
Multiply straight across. To divide, multiply by the reciprocal of the second fraction. Divide out common factors.
3
Add and subtract
Rewrite each fraction with the LCD, then combine the numerators over the LCD. Put a subtracted numerator in parentheses.
4
Complex fractions
Multiply the top and bottom of the big fraction by the LCD of all the small fractions inside it.

💭 Notice: Find the domain from the original expression, before you cancel. A factor you divide out still makes the original denominator zero.

Real-World Context

Cost per student on a field trip

A school charters a bus for $600 and pays $15 per student for museum tickets. With x students, the average cost per student is a rational expression.

Example: Average cost = (600 + 15x)/x. With 40 students, (600 + 600)/40 = $30 per student. With 60 students, it drops to 1500/60 = $25. The domain leaves out x = 0: an average for zero students makes no sense.

Worked Example · Lowest Terms

Write (x2 − 9)/(x2 + x − 6) in lowest terms

1
Factor
x2 − 9 = (x − 3)(x + 3) and x2 + x − 6 = (x + 3)(x − 2)
2
Domain
The original denominator is 0 when x = −3 or x = 2.
3
Divide out x + 3
(x − 3)/(x − 2), with x ≠ −3, 2

💭 Common slip: You cannot cancel the x2s. They are terms, not factors. Only whole factors divide out.

Worked Example · Adding

Add 3/(x − 1) + 2/(x + 2)

1
Find the LCD
The denominators share nothing, so the LCD is (x − 1)(x + 2).
2
Rewrite each fraction
3(x + 2)/((x − 1)(x + 2)) + 2(x − 1)/((x − 1)(x + 2))
3
Combine the numerators
3x + 6 + 2x − 2 = 5x + 4, so the sum is (5x + 4)/((x − 1)(x + 2))

💭 Common slip: Never add the denominators. 3/(x − 1) + 2/(x + 2) is not 5/(2x + 1).

Worked Example · Complex Fractions

Simplify (1/x + 1)/(1/x − 1)

1
Find the inside LCD
The only small denominator is x.
2
Multiply top and bottom by x
x(1/x + 1) = 1 + x and x(1/x − 1) = 1 − x
3
Write the result
(1 + x)/(1 − x), with x ≠ 0, 1

💭 Common slip: Multiply every term by x, including the 1s. x(1/x + 1) is 1 + x, not 1 + 1.

Your Turn — Guided Practice

Now you try, with me right here

Work each one, then tap your answer.

Question 1 — What is the domain of (x + 5)/(x2 − 4)?

Here's why: Only the denominator matters. x2 − 4 = 0 when x = 2 or x = −2. A zero on top, at x = −5, is allowed.

Question 2 — Multiply and simplify (x/3) · (6/x2).

Here's why: (x · 6)/(3 · x2) = 6x/(3x2). Divide out 3 and x to get 2/x. 2x puts the leftover x on top instead of the bottom.

Mini Knowledge Test

Show what you know — 5 quick questions

Question 1
Write (2x2 − 8)/(x2 + 4x + 4) in lowest terms.
Here's why: Top: 2(x − 2)(x + 2). Bottom: (x + 2)2. One factor of x + 2 divides out. The second choice drops the 2, the third divides out both copies of x + 2, and the last “cancels” the x2 terms, which are not factors.
Question 2
Divide: (x2 − 1)/(x + 3) ÷ (x − 1)/(x2 − 9)
Here's why: Flip the second fraction and multiply: (x − 1)(x + 1)/(x + 3) · (x − 3)(x + 3)/(x − 1) = (x + 1)(x − 3). The last choice multiplies without flipping, and the others put a factor in the wrong place.
Question 3
Subtract: 5/(x + 2) − 3/(x − 2)
Here's why: 5(x − 2) − 3(x + 2) = 5x − 10 − 3x − 6 = 2x − 16. 2x − 4 forgets to distribute the minus sign to the 6, 8x − 4 adds instead of subtracting, and 2 subtracts only the numerators without rewriting.
Question 4
What is the domain of (x + 1)/(x2 − 5x)?
Here's why: x2 − 5x = x(x − 5) is 0 at x = 0 and x = 5. Forgetting the factor x leaves out 0, −1 comes from the numerator, and −5 flips the sign.
Question 5
Simplify (2/x − 3)/(4/x + 1).
Here's why: Multiply the top and bottom by x: x(2/x − 3) = 2 − 3x and x(4/x + 1) = 4 + x. −1/5 drops the x entirely, and the other two multiply only some of the terms by x.
